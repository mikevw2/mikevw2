# Low-Risk Product Selection for a US Dropshipping Store Launching November 2026 (Profitable by January 2027)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: product/niche selection only (not store build, ads, or legal entity). Sources are a mix of primary (FTC, CPSC, USPTO, USPS/FedEx, Shopify's own merchant-sales data) and vendor/affiliate content (supplier directories, tool vendors). Vendor-sourced claims are labelled as such throughout.

---

## Key Question 1: What product-selection criteria minimize risk for a hands-off operator?

### Takeaway
The evidence converges on a filter set: small/light, non-fragile, non-electronic, no batteries/liquids/consumables, no regulated category (supplements, cosmetics, medical, children's products, food, safety gear), no sizing dependency, no third-party IP, advertisable on Meta/TikTok, stocked in a US warehouse or produced by US print-on-demand (POD), and priced so that contribution margin after shipping, fees, refunds and ads is still positive. The oft-cited "$25–$80 price band" is NOT confirmed by any primary source; what the sources actually support is "work backward from break-even ROAS," and a 3x cost multiple is a starting point that several sources now call insufficient on its own.

### Cited Findings

**Physical / logistics criteria**
- Big or heavy products erode margins via shipping, need special packaging, and have higher damage risk; fragile products (e.g., glass) break during long shipping and rough handling — [Do Dropshipping (affiliate site)](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)
- Fragile items "require more protective packaging, which adds to the overall weight and increases shipping costs"; recommends a first product not be fragile — [NextSmartShip (3PL vendor)](https://www.nextsmartship.com/?p=11879)
- A seller checklist favors light, compact items because "the larger the item, the more you (or your customers) will have to pay for shipping" — [Do Dropshipping pricing guide](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-pricing-strategy/)
- Customers in 2026 expect delivery within 5–10 days; 3–4 week overseas delivery is "largely unacceptable now" — [Extuitive (agency blog, Apr 2026)](https://extuitive.com/articles/is-dropshipping-profitable)
- Spocket shipping to US 2–7 days, Zendrop 5–12 days, AliExpress 15–45 days (vendor-adjacent guide) — [Unilink Spocket guide 2026](https://www.unilink.us/blog/spocket-dropshipping-guide-2026)

**Electronics / batteries / liquids / safety**
- Electronics (batteries, razors, phones, security cameras): you can't test before shipping and the dropshipper is liable for faults, returns, and possible injuries — [Do Dropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)
- Products with rechargeable batteries or wireless functions "may also face additional documentation and carrier restrictions" — [USAdrop (supplier vendor)](https://usadrop.com/?p=25977)
- Safety equipment (helmets, mouth guards, pads): a failure can cause injury and lawsuits; waterproof products: ratings often unverified, failures damage customer property — [Do Dropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)

**Regulated categories**
- Children's products: for goods made abroad, the importer must issue a Children's Product Certificate (CPC) based on testing by a CPSC-accepted third-party lab, completed before entry/distribution; a foreign manufacturer's certificate cannot simply be passed along; periodic (annual) retesting is required — [CPSC CPC FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Children%E2%80%99s-Product-Certificate); [CPSC third-party testing FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Third-Party-Testing/FAQs-Certification-and-Third-Party-Testing-OLD)
- CPSC has no guidance that addresses dropshipping specifically; a Shopify community thread (informal, not a regulator) states dropshipping does not remove compliance obligations and estimates $100–$500 per test (unverified) — [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/need-information-on-cpc-childrens-products-certificate/575095)
- Healthcare products: Meta bans before/after ads; the guide also advises against consumables (weight-loss pills, vitamins); cosmetics: unknown ingredients can cause allergic reactions; food: "no real knowledge of their ingredients" — [Do Dropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)
- Meta restricted categories such as health and finance "come with extra restrictions and may need written approval"; Meta rejects health copy using "cure," "guarantee," "rapid results" — [Bigeye agency](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/rejected-meta-ads-6-common-causes); [bir.ch](https://bir.ch/blog/what-can-you-advertise-on-meta)

**Sizing / apparel**
- Clothing and shoes: sizing confusion (especially Asian size charts) leads to many returns — [Do Dropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)
- "A lot of people will order the wrong size, or even worse order multiple and return all but one" — [MyWifeQuitHerJob](https://mywifequitherjob.com/best-dropshipping-products/)

**Shipping-promise compliance (hands-off operator risk)**
- FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR 435): seller must have a reasonable basis to ship within the advertised time or within 30 days if none stated; if delayed, must get consent or promptly refund (actual refunds, not store credit) — [FTC business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/documents/bus02-business-guide-mail-and-telephone-order-merchandise-rule); [FTC rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/rules/rulemaking-regulatory-reform-proceedings/mail-internet-or-telephone-order)
- Fashion Nova paid $9.3M after FTC alleged missed ship dates and gift cards instead of refunds — [Frankfurt Kurnit advertising law blog](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102g5bv/ftc-gets-record-settlement-for-mail-order-rule-violations)
- Meta has no written ban on dropshipping, but flags stores whose "shipping is slow," "site appears untrustworthy," or ads mislead; "bad fulfillment practices" or a pattern of complaints can restrict accounts — [Agrowth (agency)](https://agrowth.io/blogs/facebook-ads/facebook-dropshipping-ban); [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/what-is-a-meta-commerce-policy-violation/)

**Price band and margin**
- 3x cost is a "starting point for low-ticket items"; one tutorial recommends 3x for paid ads, 2–2.5x for organic — [TrueProfit](https://trueprofit.io/blog/how-to-price-dropshipping-products); [ZIK Analytics](https://www.zikanalytics.com/academy/shopify/tips-and-tricks-to-increase-sales-shopify/how-to-price-dropshipping-products-shopify-dropshipping-tutorial)
- The 3x rule is "dangerously outdated in 2026"; ad spend often eats 30–50% of revenue alongside payment fees, refunds, shipping overruns — [EarnifyHub (calculator vendor)](https://www.earnifyhub.com/blog/dropshipping/dropshipping-profit-margin-calculator)
- Worked example: $12 cost / $49 price = 75% gross margin but ~15% net after $20 CAC — [Copyfy (tool vendor, Dutch)](https://www.copyfy.io/nl/blog/dropshipping-producten-hoge-winstmarge)
- Break-even ROAS = Selling Price / Profit per unit; $40 product with $10 costs → 1.33x — [Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-your-product-margins-should-dictate-your-ad-budget/)
- Stripe US card fee: 2.9% + $0.30 — cited in [Do Dropshipping pricing guide](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-pricing-strategy/)
- Branded/private-label can reach 60–70% gross margin vs 15–25% for AliExpress arbitrage; realistic starting ad capital ~$1,000 "buys data more than guaranteed profit" (vendor blog, no underlying source) — [Unil.ink](https://unil.ink/blog/how-to-start-dropshipping-2026)
- Niche guide AOV bands: home organization $25–$100, pet products $20–$80 (vendor calculator site) — [EcomCalcTools niche guide](https://ecomcalctools.com/blog/dropshipping-niche-guide/)

**Tariff / sourcing criteria (2026 reality)**
- De minimis ($800 duty-free) ended for China/HK on May 2, 2025 (EO 14256), for all countries Aug 29, 2025; CBP made the suspension indefinite June 2026; statutory commercial exemption disappears July 1, 2027 — [Do Dropshipping tariffs page](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-tariffs/); [ASI Central](https://members.asicentral.com/news/industry-news/august-2025/de-minimis-tariff-exemption-ends-permanently-for-shipments-under-800/); [CMGM](https://www.cmgm.net/us-de-minimis-exemption-ended-2026-china/)
- Per-parcel formal entry adds roughly $4–$25 in entry/broker/bond fees plus 2–5 day delay (supplier directory) — [SaleHoo](https://salehoo.com/learn/directories-for-the-best-dropshipping-wholesale-suppliers)
- Postal informal-entry delayed-compliance window ends Oct 21, 2026; from Oct 22 shipments subject to Chapter 99 duties generally need formal entry unless filer is in CBP's Entry Type 13 test — [Epic Sourcing guide](https://www.epicsourcing.co/epic-guide/dropshipping-from-china-to-usa-complete-guide-2026)
- "US-based supplier" means three different things: US address with overseas stock (10–21+ days), vs US 3PL warehouse (3–7 days) — [SaleHoo US suppliers guide](https://www.salehoo.com/learn/usa-dropship-suppliers)

### Inferences
- A hands-off operator's biggest hidden risks are compliance and refund exposure, not product popularity: the FTC 30-day rule plus Meta's fulfillment-quality enforcement means any supplier that cannot reliably ship inside the advertised window is disqualifying. US-warehouse or US-POD sourcing is therefore a hard criterion, not a nice-to-have, given the de minimis changes.
- The "$25–$80" band is a reasonable working range but is unsupported as a rule. The defensible version: price must cover (supplier cost + US shipping + ~3% payment fees + refund reserve) and still leave a break-even ROAS of ≤1.5x–2x. At typical US-warehouse costs of $8–$20 (Zendrop examples in Q5), that implies retail ≥$30–$45.
- Children's products should be excluded entirely for a hands-off store because the CPC/testing duty sits on the importer and nobody has clarified whether a dropshipper is the importer of record.

### Gaps
- No primary source validates "$25–$80" specifically; the Ecommerce Fastlane "211 products" tier analysis is paywalled, so the price-tier-to-ad-platform mapping could not be verified.
- No official Meta English-language prohibited-products page was retrievable; restricted-category details come from agency blogs.

**Chinese New Year 2027 (restock risk)**
- CNY is Saturday Feb 6, 2027; about 11 days earlier than 2026, compressing the year-end production runway — [Drip Capital](https://www.dripcapital.com/resources/blog/chinese-new-year-2027); [SEKO Logistics](https://www.sekologistics.com/en/resource-hub/knowledge-hub/lunar-new-year-complete-supply-chain-planning-guide-for-asia-sourcing/)
- Factories stop meaningful production 1–2 weeks before and take 1–2 weeks after; many stop accepting new orders 6–8 weeks before CNY; pre-holiday slowdown expected ~Jan 9–16, 2027; reopening ~Feb 20–Mar 6 at ~35% capacity; full recovery ~Mar 6–20, 2027; up to 25% of workers may not return — [SEKO Logistics](https://www.sekologistics.com/en/resource-hub/knowledge-hub/lunar-new-year-complete-supply-chain-planning-guide-for-asia-sourcing/); [Inventory Hero](https://www.inventoryhero.ai/blog/chinese-new-year-amazon-fba)

- Post-CNY quality problems are common enough to warrant inspection guidance — [V-Trust](https://chs.v-trust.com/ar/blog/post-cny-quality-issues)

### Inferences
- For a Nov 2026 launch, the realistic paid-promotion window for Christmas gifting is Nov 20–Dec 15 (ship-by Dec 15–17 for ground). Anything China-direct ordered after ~Dec 10 will not arrive by Christmas, and China restocks ordered after ~mid-December will not land until March 2027. This makes US-warehouse/POD sourcing the only way to sell continuously through the January 2027 window.
- Categories that work for BOTH windows (gift in December, "new year" in January) are the lowest-risk bets: organization/planners/journals, home-fitness accessories, cozy home textiles, pet enrichment, and coloring/craft books.
- Shopify's conflicting growth lists and 2024/2025 date mixing mean its specific growth percentages should be treated as directional, not planning inputs.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Google Trends, Exploding Topics or a dated TikTok Creative Center Q4 2025 snapshot through search; these must be pulled live from the tools (suggested queries: "fitness planner," "habit tracker," "resistance bands," "desk organizer," "coloring book," Dec–Mar across 3+ years, US filter).
- No published Printful 2026 bestseller ranking was found; Printify's figures are its own marketing.
- No official UPS 2026 holiday deadlines found as of Oct 2026.

## Key Question 3: Categories with historically high vs low return/chargeback/dissatisfaction rates

### Takeaway
Apparel/footwear are consistently the highest-return categories (roughly 20–40% online, fit-driven); electronics are mid-range (8–20%) but carry defect/liability and ad-policy risk; beauty is lowest (4–12%) mainly because opened products cannot be returned — not because it is safe to dropship. The NRF/Happy Returns 2025 headline rates are 15.8% overall and 19.3% online. Category-level numbers come from logistics/returns vendors with inconsistent methodologies.

### Cited Findings
- NRF/Happy Returns 2025: forecast 15.8% return rate ($849.9B) in 2025, down from 16.9% ($890B) in 2024; 19.3% of online sales expected to be returned — [Retail TouchPoints on NRF report](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/?p=154866); [AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025); [NRF/Happy Returns PDF](https://a-us.storyblok.com/f/1021220/x/6e22cae2b7/2025-retail-returns-landscape.pdf) (PDF too large to parse; category breakdown unconfirmed)
- Apparel 20–40%, footwear 17–30%, home & furniture 15–23%, accessories & jewelry 12–15%, beauty & personal care 4–12% (lowest, partly because opened products are refused) — [Claimlane](https://www.claimlane.com/resources/blog/average-ecommerce-return-rates); [Red Stag Fulfillment](https://redstagfulfillment.com/average-return-rates-for-ecommerce/)
- Electronics 8–15% in one benchmark, 15–20% in another, 15–25% on Amazon, 8.28% in a ClickPost dataset; electronics returns driven by damage/inaccurate descriptions, apparel by fit — [ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/en-us/blog/most-returned-products-online); [Red Stag Amazon data](https://redstagfulfillment.com/how-many-amazon-orders-get-returned-each-year/); [TrueProfit](https://trueprofit.io/blog/rate-of-return)
- 26% of US consumers returned clothing bought online in 2025 (share of consumers, not of sales); Coresight 2023: 24.4% US online apparel return rate — [Ringly](https://ringly.io/blog/ecommerce-return-statistics-2026)
- Fit/sizing linked to up to 70% of apparel returns (vendor analysis) — [Size.ly](https://www.size.ly/blog/ecommerce-return-statistics)
- Dropshipping-specific estimate: 5–15% returns depending on category; "each return can wipe out shipping, product and acquisition costs" — [Extuitive](https://extuitive.com/articles/is-dropshipping-profitable)
- Watches: "heavily crowded, and many watch stores are associated with scams" (chargeback/dissatisfaction proxy) — [Do Dropshipping](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)
- Supplier-side evidence of dissatisfaction: a USAdrop reviewer (Dec 2025, 3-year customer) reported 24 customer orders returned, forced refunds and lost ad spend due to missed response deadlines; the app shows 83% 5-star and 12% 1-star — [Shopify App Store reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/1776740)

### Inferences
- Lowest-return, lowest-liability product shapes are: non-sized, non-powered, non-consumable, visually predictable items (organization, textiles, pet enrichment, stationery, craft/coloring). Jewelry is mid-return but has well-known quality/tarnish dissatisfaction risk in dropshipping; "gadget" products inherit electronics' defect rate plus the inability to test.
- Beauty's low return rate is a trap: it is low because returns are refused, while the category is regulated and ad-restricted.
- For planning, assume 5–10% returns for the shortlisted non-apparel categories and 20%+ for anything sized.

### Gaps
- No dropshipping-specific chargeback-rate data by category was found; NRF's category breakdown could not be extracted (PDF too large).
- No source quantifies jewelry-quality or "gadget" dissatisfaction rates specifically for dropshipping.

## Key Question 4: Saturation vs underserved niches; "winning product" churn in 2026

### Takeaway
Every source — all of them vendors or agencies — agrees generic "AliExpress product on a logo-less Shopify store" is effectively dead because of de minimis loss, Meta CPM inflation (~60% above 2022 per one unsourced claim) and faster copying via AI research tools. The defensible edge has shifted to niche identity, creative testing, and US fulfillment speed rather than product discovery. Watches, generic gadgets, mainstream commodities and anything findable locally are explicitly called saturated.

### Cited Findings
- Generic store model called "effectively dead" (search saturation, ad cost inflation, tariffs); Meta CPMs in most ecommerce niches ~60% above 2022 so a $300 test now costs ~$1,000 (no underlying source shown) — [Unil.ink (vendor)](https://unil.ink/blog/how-to-start-dropshipping-2026)
- "Enter a product too late and you compete in a saturated market with collapsing margins" — [TrendTrack (tool vendor)](https://www.trendtrack.io/blog-post/ai-to-identify-profitable-dropshipping-products-in-2026)
- 15–25% of monthly ad spend wasted on fatigued creative; cold-audience performance drops past frequency ~2.5; Triple Whale fatigue rule: flag when 2+ metrics fall over 7 days and performance is >15% below 30-day baseline — [Segwise (tool vendor)](https://segwise.ai/blog/ai-marketing-tools-dropshipping-2026)
- 80–90% of new dropshippers fail in year one; 1–2% reach large scale (sourcing unclear) — [NextSky](https://nextsky.co/blogs/dropshipping/is-dropshipping-worth-it/)
- Still profitable in 2026 but depends on niche selection, supplier quality, marketing execution — [Extuitive](https://extuitive.com/articles/is-dropshipping-profitable)
- Saturated/avoid: watches; mainstream products customers can buy locally; "avoid overly saturated products (unless you have a unique marketing angle)" — [Do Dropshipping products to avoid](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/); [Do Dropshipping niches to avoid](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-niches-to-avoid/)
- Organic-first validation: "don't spend $1 on ads until you have 3–5 organic TikToks over 1,000 views"; "you don't compete on price, you compete on identity" — [Dropified (vendor, claims to summarize Reddit without links)](https://www.dropified.com/blog/explore-the-future-of-dropshipping-reddit-2023-insights/)
- Heavy ad competition / IP-risk categories: brand logos, luxury fashion, electronics, character merchandise — [Do Dropshipping trademark guide](https://dodropshipping.com/how-to-check-if-a-product-is-trademarked/)

### Inferences
- "Winning product" churn: if everyone uses the same AI research tools (Minea/AdSpy/TrendTrack) on the same ad libraries, a product's exclusivity window is measured in weeks. Low-risk strategy is to pick evergreen, boring categories and win on niche positioning (e.g., "dog-lovers' home organization," "teacher planners") rather than chasing viral gadgets.
- Relatively underserved by heavy dropship ad spend (inference from the saturated lists above): functional home/desk organization, stationery/planners, pet enrichment (non-consumable), craft/coloring, cozy home textiles. These are high-volume on Shopify (arts & crafts #11, bedding #14) but dominated by brands rather than dropship ad arbitrage.
- Budget consequence: with a $1,000 test now typical, a November launch has room for only 2–4 serious product tests before the Dec 15 ship cutoff; the shortlist should be pre-vetted rather than discovered via ads.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) data on Meta CPM inflation, product churn rate, or dropshipper failure rates was found.
- No actual Reddit threads surfaced via search (results were vendor blogs claiming to summarize Reddit); r/dropship, r/shopify and r/ecommerce must be searched directly for 2025–2026 firsthand accounts.

## Key Question 5: Candidate shortlist (8–12) with cost/price/margin, seasonality, competition, risk flags, sourcing

### Takeaway
Eleven candidates pass the low-risk filter. Only a few have sourced cost/retail figures (Zendrop pet feeder toy $11.21 → $44.84; generic $12 → $49 worked example); the rest carry estimated bands that are inferences and must be verified against live Spocket/Zendrop/CJ-US/Printify catalogs. POD items (tote bags, journals, beanies, blankets, hoodies) have zero inventory risk and US production, at the cost of lower gross margin.

## Key Question 6: Categories to avoid, and intellectual-property risk checks

### Takeaway
Avoid-list is consistent across regulators and practitioner guides: electronics/batteries, liquids/cosmetics/supplements/food, children's products, safety gear, weapons/knives, fragile goods, heavy/bulky goods, sized apparel/footwear, watches, waterproof claims, counterfeit/licensed/character merchandise, and anything Meta won't run. IP checks: USPTO Trademark Search (free; word-mark exact search; design search codes; no reverse image search), plus WIPO Global Brand Database and EUIPO; the takedown chain on POD platforms runs DMCA → listing removal → account freeze → catalog wipe, and fighting it is slow and expensive.

### Cited Findings

**Avoid list (with reasons)**
- Big/heavy; not advertisable on Facebook (unregulated supplements, adult, weapons, tobacco, drugs, surveillance equipment); counterfeit/copyrighted; watches; dangerous (knives — customs seizure); fragile (glass); clothing & shoes (sizing); electronics (batteries, razors, phones, cameras — untestable, liability); mainstream commodities; healthcare (before/after ads banned; consumables unsafe); safety equipment (helmets, mouth guards, pads — injury lawsuits); cosmetics (allergic reactions; makeup brushes suggested as safer); waterproof products (unverified ratings) — [Do Dropshipping products to avoid](https://dodropshipping.com/products-to-avoid-dropshipping/)
- Seasonal one-shot items (e.g., Halloween costumes) carry demand risk — [Syncee](https://syncee.com/blog/tips-tutorials/best-dropshipping-products/)
- Children's products trigger CPC + third-party lab testing + annual periodic testing, with the importer responsible — [CPSC CPC FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Children%E2%80%99s-Product-Certificate)
- Meta prohibited: weapons, illegal drugs, certain adult products; restricted (health, finance) may need written approval — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/what-is-a-meta-commerce-policy-violation/); [bir.ch](https://bir.ch/blog/what-can-you-advertise-on-meta)
- Rechargeable batteries/wireless products face documentation and carrier restrictions — [USAdrop](https://usadrop.com/?p=25977)
- Shopify lists socks, posters/artwork, candles, lip stain, matcha, power-tool accessories as no longer trending — [Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com/blog/trending-products)

**IP / trademark / patent checks**
- USPTO Trademark Search: free; wordmark exact-match search for pre-filing clearance; multi-word entries are split into separate terms (use field tags); no reverse image search but six-digit design search codes find marks with similar design elements; filter live/dead; export results — [USPTO federal trademark searching handout](https://Www.Uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/TM-FederalTrademarkSearching-GettingStarted-handout.pdf)
- Check USPTO, then WIPO Global Brand Database and EUIPO eSearch plus; "a search is a first screen, not legal clearance"; supplier catalogs can include fakes and selling them creates liability; highest-risk categories: brand logos, luxury fashion, electronics, character merchandise (Disney, Marvel) — [Do Dropshipping trademark guide](https://dodropshipping.com/how-to-check-if-a-product-is-trademarked/)
- Copyright protects original creative works automatically on creation; trademark protects brand identifiers (names, logos, slogans) — [Printify copyright vs trademark](https://printify.com/knowledge-hub/copyright-vs-trademark-print-on-demand/)
- Takedown chain: DMCA takedown → listing removal → account freeze → catalog wiped; "filing a takedown is cheap and instant for the rights holder; fighting one is slow and expensive for you" — [Do Dropshipping POD protection guide](https://dodropshipping.com/how-to-protect-print-on-demand-designs/)
- Trademark registration for your own brand name is advised to protect a dropshipping brand (vendor) — [Trademark Engine](https://www.trademarkengine.com/blog/how-to-protect-a-dropshipping-brand-with-a-trademark/)

### Inferences
- Common takedown triggers for the shortlist: slogans/phrases on totes, hoodies, beanies, journals (check USPTO wordmark search for each phrase, including common "funny" phrases which are frequently registered in Class 25); sports-team, university, band or character references in designs; product photos lifted from Amazon brand listings; and organizer/pet-toy shapes that copy a brand's registered design patent (search USPTO design patents via Patent Public Search by product name + "ornamental design").
- Operating rule for a hands-off store: use only original or openly licensed artwork, keep a per-design clearance log (USPTO + WIPO screenshot), and never use brand names in titles, tags or ads.

### Gaps
- No source found covering USPTO design-patent search workflow (Patent Public Search) or DMCA counter-notice procedure specifically for dropshippers; one guide's statement that design patents require a "functional purpose" is incorrect (design patents cover ornamental appearance) and should not be relied on.
- Meta's official English prohibited/restricted commerce policy page was not retrievable; verify directly at transparency.meta.com before ad launch.

## Selection Criteria Used (summary for the report writer)

Hard filters (any failure = exclude): (1) no electronics, batteries, wireless, or liquids; (2) no supplements, cosmetics, food, medical, safety equipment, weapons; (3) no children's products (CPSC CPC); (4) no sized apparel/footwear except POD hoodies as a flagged exception; (5) non-fragile (no glass/ceramic/acrylic); (6) ships flat or small, under roughly 2 lb (inference); (7) available from a US warehouse or US POD provider with ≤7-day delivery; (8) advertisable on Meta/TikTok without restricted-category approval; (9) no third-party trademarks, characters or licensed designs; (10) supplier cost allows retail ≥ ~3x cost AND break-even ROAS ≤ ~1.5–2x after shipping and 3% payment fees.

Soft scores: demand in both Dec 2026 and Jan 2027 windows; evergreen baseline; Shopify category volume/growth signal; expected return rate ≤10%; ability to niche the positioning; bundle potential to reach $30–$60 ticket.

Launch-timeline constraints that drove the choices: USPS Ground Advantage ship-by Dec 17 and FedEx Ground Economy Dec 15 for Christmas; CNY Feb 6, 2027 with factory slowdown from ~Jan 9–16 and recovery into March, so no China-direct restocks for the January window.
