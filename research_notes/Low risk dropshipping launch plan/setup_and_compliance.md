# Legal, Tax, Platform and Payments Setup for a Low-Involvement Idaho Dropshipping Store (state as of 2026-10-08)

Scope note: all figures below carry the date they were seen (2026-10-08 unless stated). Official/primary sources are preferred; where only vendor blogs or forum posts were available this is flagged. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.

## Key Question 1: Business entity — Idaho LLC vs sole proprietorship; filing cost, annual report, EIN, registered agent, local licensing

### Takeaway
An Idaho single-member LLC costs $100 to form online (one-time), has a $0 annual report, needs no paid registered agent if the owner has an Idaho street address, and gets a free EIN from the IRS — total hard cost ~$100 and ~1 week. Given that cost, the LLC is worth it for a dropshipping store mainly because (a) Shopify Payments/banks verify against the EIN and business registration, (b) it separates chargeback/consumer liability from personal assets, and (c) it is a disregarded entity for tax so there is no extra federal return. Twin Falls has no general business license, but a home occupation may need a zoning permit.

### Cited Findings
- Idaho LLC Certificate of Organization: $100 base fee; paper filings add a $20 manual processing fee ($120 total); "Most business filings can be completed online at sosbiz.idaho.gov," which avoids the $20 fee. Paper forms submitted without the fee are rejected. (seen 2026-10-08) — [Idaho SOS Business Forms](https://sos.idaho.gov/business-forms/); [Idaho SOS LLC Certificate of Organization form](https://sos.idaho.gov/CORP/forms/LLC/LLC%20Cert%20org.pdf)
- Idaho LLC Annual Report: no fee listed on the SOS forms page; annual reports are filed online at SOSBiz — [Idaho SOS Business Forms](https://sos.idaho.gov/business-forms/). Third-party guides agree the annual report is $0 — [ZenBusiness Idaho filing fees (updated Feb 2026)](https://www.zenbusiness.com/idaho-filing-fees/); [LLC University Idaho LLC costs](https://www.llcuniversity.com/idaho-llc/costs/). One aggregator (howtostartanllc.com) claims $100/$120 for the annual report; this conflicts with the SOS page and other guides and appears wrong — [HowToStartAnLLC Idaho cost](https://howtostartanllc.com/idaho-llc/cost-to-form)
- Annual report is due by the end of the LLC's anniversary month each year starting the year after formation (e.g., approved March 15 → due March 31 the following year); Idaho charges no late fee, but an LLC not filed within 60 days of the due date may be administratively dissolved — [LLC University Idaho LLC costs](https://www.llcuniversity.com/idaho-llc/costs/)
- Certificate of Assumed Business Name (DBA): $25 base + $20 manual fee if on paper — [Idaho SOS Business Forms](https://sos.idaho.gov/business-forms/)
- Expedited service on some filings: $40 expedited / $100 same-day (shown for name registration; "some filings may be eligible," check page 2 of the form) — [Idaho SOS Business Forms](https://sos.idaho.gov/business-forms/)
- Certificate of Existence (good standing): $10 via the SOS certificate request form (third-party figure) — [HowToStartAnLLC Idaho cost](https://howtostartanllc.com/idaho-llc/cost-to-form)
- Registered agent: every Idaho LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical Idaho street address (no P.O. Box); an individual over 18 who lives in Idaho and is available during business hours may serve, including the owner; the address becomes public record — [LLC University: be your own registered agent in Idaho](https://www.llcuniversity.com/can-i-be-my-own-registered-agent-in-idaho/); [Zenind Idaho registered agent](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/idaho-registered-agent-for-llcs-requirements-duties-and-zenind-support)
- EIN: free from the IRS; the online application requires the principal business in the US, the applicant to be the "responsible party" (or authorized representative) with that party's SSN/ITIN, must be completed in one session (expires after 15 minutes idle); IRS warns against sites that charge for an EIN. Form the LLC first, then apply — [IRS: Apply for an EIN online](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
- Idaho individual income tax is a flat 5.3% (effective Jan 1, 2025; cut from 5.695%); a single-member LLC's profit flows to the owner's return — [Idaho State Tax Commission: Individual Income Tax Basics](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/income-tax/individual-income/online-guide/); [Idaho Capital Sun, Mar 6 2025](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2025/03/06/idaho-gov-brad-little-signs-states-largest-income-tax-cut-into-law/)
- City of Twin Falls does not issue a blanket business license; only certain business types need one, and the city provides an online form to check; a home occupation "may require a special use permit from the Planning and Zoning Commission" (Planning & Zoning: (208) 735-7267) — [City of Twin Falls Business Licenses](https://tfid.org/131/Business-Licenses)
- Idaho SBDC: an online business must register a business name/entity with the Idaho SOS; if it involves sales, a sales tax permit is needed; home-based businesses must be run by a full-time resident and be a secondary use of the home; some cities require a special home-business license — [Idaho SBDC FAQ on home businesses](https://idahosbdc.org/sbdc_faq/i-plan-to-start-my-business-in-my-home-are-there-special-regulations-i-need-to-know-about/)
- Shopify Payments: for sole proprietors and single-member LLCs, "the owner is designated as the account representative"; EIN-based business types must enter the registered name exactly as on IRS documents (CP575 / 147C) — [Shopify Help: US requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/united-states/requirements)

### Inferences
- Total one-time entity cost: $100 (LLC online) + $0 (EIN) + $0 (self as registered agent) + $0 (annual report) = ~$100, versus $0 for a sole proprietorship. The marginal cost of the LLC is trivial relative to its liability separation and the cleaner KYC story with Shopify Payments and banks.
- Sole-prop route only makes sense if the owner wants zero paperwork; but the Idaho small-seller sales-tax exemption (see Q2) applies only to individuals/sole proprietors under $5,000 — a store aiming to be "profitable by January" will blow past that anyway.
- Timeline: SOSBiz online LLC filings are typically processed in days (no official SLA found); EIN is immediate online. Budget one week for entity + EIN + bank account in October 2026 to be safe for a November launch.
- Using the home address as registered office makes it public; a paid registered agent (~$50–150/yr, not sourced here) or a non-residential address is the privacy trade-off.

### Gaps
- No official SOS processing-time SLA for online LLC filings was found.
- Whether Twin Falls requires a home-occupation permit for a no-inventory, no-customer-visits online store was not confirmed; the city FAQ says "may." A phone call to Planning & Zoning is the resolution step.
- Idaho 2026 bill H0589 amends the income-tax rate section; I could not confirm whether it changed the 5.3% rate.

## Key Question 2: Idaho sales tax registration and rules; marketplace/Shopify collection; economic nexus elsewhere

### Takeaway
Idaho sales tax is 6%; the seller's permit is free and obtained online at tax.idaho.gov/ibr, then filed/paid via a free TAP account. A Shopify store (not a marketplace) must collect Idaho tax itself on Idaho-destination orders; Shopify Tax calculates it but does not file. Other states' economic nexus (mostly $100,000 of sales into that state, some still 200 transactions) is irrelevant until the store is doing roughly six figures into a single state — a tiny store only needs to collect in Idaho, but should monitor per-state totals and watch the handful of states with transaction-count tests.

### Cited Findings
- Idaho sales tax rate is 6%; use tax is also 6% — [Idaho STC Sales and Use Tax Basics Guide](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/online-guide/)
- Applying for an Idaho seller's permit is free; a regular permit covers all sales and is valid until cancelled — [Idaho STC: Who Needs a Seller's Permit](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/permits/who-needs-a-sellers-permit/)
- Steps for online sellers: register for a seller's permit at tax.idaho.gov/ibr; collect and forward tax; after the permit arrives by mail, register for a free Taxpayer Access Point (TAP) account to file and pay online; keep records of purchases and sales — [Idaho STC: Online Sellers Take These Steps](https://tax.idaho.gov/?p=165100)
- Since June 1, 2019 Idaho requires marketplace facilitators to collect and forward Idaho sales tax on third-party sales; sellers should ask the facilitator for written verification; if the facilitator is not collecting, the seller must report it under its own permit — [Idaho STC: Online Sellers Take These Steps](https://tax.idaho.gov/?p=165100)
- Idaho economic nexus for out-of-state retailers: more than $100,000 in Idaho sales (the "Who Needs a Permit" page says "in any 12-month period"; the Online Sellers guide says "current or previous calendar year" — the two state pages phrase the period differently) — [Idaho STC: Who Needs a Seller's Permit](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/permits/who-needs-a-sellers-permit/); [Idaho STC Online Sellers Guide](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/online-sellers/online-guide/)
- Idaho small-seller exemption (effective July 1, 2025): Idaho residents operating as individuals or sole proprietorships with cumulative gross sales at or under $5,000 in the current or previous calendar year need no permit; must get one within 30 days of exceeding — [Idaho STC press release](https://tax.idaho.gov/pressrelease/new-law-means-idaho-small-sellers-wont-have-to-charge-sales-tax/)
- Economic nexus exists in all 45 sales-tax states plus DC; most thresholds are $100,000 in sales over the current or previous calendar year; the 200-transaction test is being phased out (recent removals include Alaska, Utah, Illinois, Kentucky); sources differ on how many jurisdictions still use a transaction test (18 vs. "29 states now sales-only") — [Kintsugi Economic Nexus by State 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus); [Numeral 2026 Economic Nexus Handbook](https://www.numeral.com/blog/economic-nexus); [TaxCloud 2026 Nexus Thresholds PDF (as of Mar 5 2026)](https://taxcloud.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026_Sales_Tax_Nexus_Thresholds_TaxCloud.pdf)
- Example 2026 thresholds (TaxCloud table, Mar 2026): Alabama $250,000 (prior year); Arizona/Colorado $100,000; California $500,000; Connecticut $100,000 AND 200 transactions (12 months ending Sept 30); Florida $100,000 (prior year); Georgia $100,000 OR 200 transactions; New York $500,000 AND 100 transactions (per one source) — [TaxCloud 2026 Nexus Thresholds PDF](https://taxcloud.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026_Sales_Tax_Nexus_Thresholds_TaxCloud.pdf); [Numeral 2026 Economic Nexus Handbook](https://www.numeral.com/blog/economic-nexus)
- Physical presence (e.g., inventory in a 3PL warehouse) creates nexus independently of economic thresholds — [beancount.io nexus guide (Jul 2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/22/sales-tax-nexus-2026-economic-nexus-thresholds-100k-200-transaction-trap-wayfair-guide)
- Shopify Tax: free for stores under $100,000 in global sales per calendar year; above that, 0.35% per order on standard plans (one source adds caps of $0.99/order and $5,000/state/year from Plus collateral); it calculates tax but does not register or file — [Numeral: Avalara vs Shopify Tax](https://numeral.com/blog/avalara-vs-shopify-tax); [EightX: Shopify Tax vs Avalara vs TaxJar](https://eightx.co/blog/shopify-tax-vs-avalara-vs-taxjar)
- TaxJar (Stripe-owned) 2026 pricing: Starter $39/mo, Professional $99/mo at 200 orders/mo (first increase in six years); Starter includes 2 filing credits/yr then $50/filing; Shopify App Store rating has fallen to 2.9 — [Kintsugi: TaxJar vs Avalara 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/blog/taxjar-vs-avalara.md)
- Avalara is quote-based and discontinued its Returns for Small Business program in 2024 — [Kintsugi: TaxJar vs Avalara 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/blog/taxjar-vs-avalara.md)

### Inferences
- A Shopify storefront is NOT a marketplace facilitator; Shopify collects nothing on the merchant's behalf. The store is the retailer of record and must register in Idaho (physical presence = home office) before the first Idaho sale. Register the permit in October 2026 (it arrives by mail, then TAP enrollment follows), so allow ~2 weeks.
- Because the LLC is not an individual/sole proprietor, the $5,000 small-seller exemption does not apply; register regardless.
- Practical rule for a tiny store: collect only Idaho tax at launch; enable Shopify Tax (free under $100k) with Idaho as the only registered region; review Shopify's per-state sales report quarterly. Register in another state only when its threshold is crossed (lowest-risk watch items: states with 200-transaction tests such as Connecticut/Georgia-type rules, since 200 orders of a $20 product is just $4,000 of sales).
- Dropshipping from China with no US inventory creates no physical-presence nexus; using a US 3PL or supplier US warehouse (e.g., CJ's US stock) in another state could.
- Idaho filing frequency is assigned by the Tax Commission (typically monthly/quarterly/annual based on volume); the exact assignment rule was not found — treat as a gap.

### Gaps
- Idaho filing frequency assignment thresholds not found on the pages fetched.
- Official per-state 2026 nexus table was only available via vendor compilations (TaxCloud, Kintsugi, Numeral), which disagree on NY and on counts of transaction-test states; verify any specific state on its DOR site before registering.
- Shopify Tax's exact 2026 fee caps come from vendor blogs, not Shopify's pricing page.

### Takeaway
Shopify Basic at $39/month (or $29/month billed annually), 2.9% + 30¢ online card rate via Shopify Payments and 0% extra transaction fee, plus a 3-day free trial then $1/month for 3 months, is the lowest-risk, most automation-friendly choice for a US dropshipper. ## Key Question 4: Payments and risk — Shopify Payments vs Stripe vs PayPal; why new stores get held; chargeback thresholds

### Takeaway
All three processors apply reserves to new, fast-growing, or slow-shipping merchants; Shopify's own Help Center names long delivery timelines, volume surges, elevated chargebacks and refund rates as triggers, with examples of 10% or $1,000 held for 120 days. Chargebacks cost $15 each on Shopify Payments (refunded if won), and card-network monitoring begins well under 1% (Visa VAMP ratio 0.5%; Mastercard ECP 1.5% and 100 disputes). The avoidance playbook is: ship in days not weeks (US-stocked suppliers or DDP express), tracked shipments, auto-refund fast, keep disputes near zero, verify identity/EIN/bank before launch, and enable two-step auth.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify reserves: "a temporary hold on a portion, in some cases a full amount, of transactions"; triggers include "extended billing cycles," "elevated chargeback activity," "increased refund rates," "industries with long delivery timelines (such as pre-orders)," and "significant volume surges"; types are fixed-amount (example $1,000 for 120 days) and percentage (example 10% for 120 days); terms are communicated by email; some reserves can't be appealed; reserved funds join payouts after expiry "which might take several business days" — [Shopify Help: Reserves](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/reserves)
- Shopify Payments US terms: "We, in our discretion, will set the terms of any Reserve Account"; Shopify may charge the merchant's payment method if balances don't cover obligations; account may be suspended for "elevated risk" — [Shopify Payments Terms of Service (US)](https://www.shopify.com/legal/terms-payments-us)
- Chargeback mechanics: bank immediately debits disputed amount plus fee; US chargeback fee is $15; merchant typically has 7–21 days to submit evidence; issuer review up to 75 days; fee returned with the amount if the merchant wins; chargeback amounts are deducted from the next payout; Shopify "isn't liable for chargebacks and isn't involved in chargeback decisions" — [Shopify Help: Chargeback process](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/chargeback-process); [Shopify Help: Chargebacks](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks)
- Network monitoring thresholds (as summarized by Shopify Help, seen 2026-10-08): Visa VAMP ratio "non-compliant" at 0.5%, "excessive" at 1.5% (count 1,500); prolonged enrollment "can restrict your use of Shopify Payments." Mastercard ECP: 100–299 disputes AND 1.5–2.99% rate, fines escalate from $1,000 (months 2–3) to $100,000+; HECM at 300+ disputes and 3%. Exit after 3 consecutive months below threshold. Merchants terminated for cause land on MATCH/VMSS lists and Shopify "generally can't process" for them — [Shopify Help: Monitoring programs](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/monitoring-programs)
- Merchant anecdotes (forum, unverified): store went from $212 day one to $10,026 day two and received a 20% hold for 120 days; another with one chargeback was told "dropshippers are an increased risk" and got a 20% hold; release typically after 90–120 days of clean processing — [Shopify Community: 20% hold](https://community.shopify.com/t/20-hold-on-all-income-from-shopify-please-help/66487); [Shopify Community: 120-day hold](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-hold-for-120-days-and-30-hold/607089); [Shopify Community: unsupportable by banking partners](https://community.shopify.com/t/my-shopify-account-has-been-deemed-unsupportable-by-shopifys-banking-partners/387045)
- PayPal reserves (official): rolling reserve is most common, example 10% for 90 days; review factors include account age, processing history, industry chargeback risk, whether you sell before shipping, delivery timeframes; PayPal recommends shipping promptly with tracking and keeping buyer complaints below 1%; reserves may be lowered or removed as business improves — [PayPal Help: What are reserves?](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-are-reserves-help393)
- Stripe: terms allow holding payout funds in reserve for up to 180 days or longer with disputes and changing the amount at any time; Stripe publishes no reserve percentage; rolling reserves commonly applied to new accounts and sudden volume increases (third-party summaries of Stripe ToS) — [Conduct Atlas: Stripe ToS fund withholding](https://conductatlas.com/platform/stripe/stripe-terms-of-service/fund-withholding-and-reserve-authority/); [Terms.law Stripe holds FAQ](https://terms.law/FAQ/payment-processors/stripe-holds-faq.html)
- Shopify Payments US verification requirements: full USD checking account at a US bank, ACH-eligible (no savings, wire-only, multi-currency or bank-imitating fintech accounts; virtual accounts have more failures) or a Shopify Balance account; individuals/sole props must give legal name, DOB, SSN/ITIN, US phone and residential address; EIN entities give registered name exactly as on IRS CP575/147C, EIN, business phone and US address; ID = unexpired passport/driver's license/state ID; EIN proof = SS-4 letter, CP575 or 147C; address proof within 6 months; documents must be original camera photos or original PDFs (no scans/screenshots) — [Shopify Help: US requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/united-states/requirements)
- Two-step authentication must be active to receive payouts; payouts may be held without it; business address must match registration paperwork; account representative must be an owner/senior executive able to bind the company — [Shopify Help: Setting up Shopify Payments](https://help.shopify.com/manual/payment-settings/shopify-payments/fraud-prevention/)
- Canceling orders flagged high-risk by Shopify's fraud analysis helps avoid fraud chargebacks; network dispute-resolution programs can auto-refund some disputes before they become chargebacks — [Shopify Help: Chargebacks](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks)

### Inferences
- Shopify Payments is the right primary processor (no extra fee, integrated fraud tools, dispute program enrollment). Add PayPal as a secondary wallet only if conversion data demands it; a second processor also means a second KYC and a second reserve exposure.
- Risk-minimizing launch pattern: (1) complete all Shopify Payments verification and bank linking BEFORE the first ad; (2) avoid day-one volume spikes (ramp ad spend gradually over the first 2–3 weeks); (3) use suppliers with US warehouses or express DDP so delivery is under ~10 days — this directly addresses the "long delivery timeline" trigger and the FTC 30-day rule; (4) set a refund-first policy (a refund costs the product; a chargeback costs product + $15 + ratio damage); (5) keep dispute ratio below 0.5% — on a store doing 200 orders/month that is 1 dispute.
- Expect a reserve anyway if sales ramp quickly; cash-flow plan should assume 10–20% of revenue may be inaccessible for up to 120 days.

### Gaps
- No official statement from Shopify on a numeric chargeback threshold that triggers a reserve; the 1% figure in forums is anecdotal.
- Shopify Payments standard US payout schedule (business days) was not captured from an official page in this pass.
- Stripe's reserve policy details come from third-party summaries of its ToS, not Stripe's page.

## Key Question 5: Required policies and consumer-protection obligations — FTC 30-day rule, refunds, privacy, terms, import/de minimis status

### Takeaway
The FTC Mail/Internet Order Rule (16 CFR 435) requires a reasonable basis to ship within the stated time or 30 days, mandatory delay notices with a free cancel option, and refunds within 7 working days of cancellation — so a China-direct dropshipper must state realistic ship times and automate delay notices/refunds. De minimis is gone: duty-free $800 treatment was suspended for China/HK on May 2 2025, globally Aug 29 2025, continued by EO in Feb 2026, and made indefinite by CBP interim final rules published June 24 2026; every parcel owes duty, and who pays depends on DDP (seller/supplier) vs DDU (customer billed by carrier). The tariff rate stack has changed repeatedly in 2026 (IEEPA struck down Feb 20 2026; Section 122 10% surcharge Feb 24–Jul 24 2026, itself held unlawful by the CIT; Section 301 remains) and must be re-checked at launch.

### Cited Findings (FTC and policies)
- Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, 16 CFR Part 435: seller must have a reasonable basis to ship within the advertised time or, if none stated, within 30 days of receiving a properly completed order (50 days if the buyer applies for in-house credit); "properly completed order" = payment tendered/authorized plus all info needed to ship; missing records create a presumption of no reasonable basis — [FTC Business Guide to the Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)
- Delay obligations: first delay notice must give a revised date (or say you can't), state the right to cancel for a full refund, provide a cost-free way to cancel; if the revised date is ≤30 days late, silence = consent; if >30 days or indefinite, the order auto-cancels unless the customer affirmatively consents; a second missed date requires a renewed notice where silence cannot count as consent — [FTC Business Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)
- Refund timing: cash/check/third-party credit card refunds within seven working days of cancellation; where seller is the creditor, within one billing cycle; refunds must be made without being asked when the seller cannot ship — [FTC Business Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule); [FTC rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)
- CCPA applicability thresholds (sources conflict): $26.625M gross revenue (inflation-adjusted Jan 1 2025) per one guide vs. $25M per another; OR buying/selling/sharing personal data of 100,000+ consumers/households; OR 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing data — [Consently CCPA 2026 checklist](https://consently.net/blog/ccpa-requirements); [Consently: how to comply with CCPA 2026](https://consently.net/blog/how-to-comply-with-ccpa)
- California CIPA (wiretap) demand letters target trackers firing before consent; statutory damages commonly cited at $5,000 per violation — [Ecommerce Fastlane: CIPA and CCPA 2026](https://ecommercefastlane.com/cipa-ccpa-us-privacy-ecommerce/)
- Baseline documents regardless of size: privacy policy plus notice at collection (cookie banner linked to policy); a privacy policy must match what pixels/chat widgets actually do — [pii.ai privacy policy requirements 2026](https://www.pii.ai/blog/privacy-policy-requirements-ecommerce-2026); [Ecommerce Fastlane: CIPA and CCPA 2026](https://ecommercefastlane.com/cipa-ccpa-us-privacy-ecommerce/)

### Cited Findings (de minimis / tariffs / who pays)
- De minimis timeline: removed for China and Hong Kong May 2 2025 (EO 14256), suspended for all countries Aug 29 2025 — [Carra Globe: US de minimis suspended 2026](https://carraglobe.com/us-de-minimis-exemption-suspended-2026/); [TariffsTool: de minimis ended 2026](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/de-minimis-exemption-ended-2026)
- February 2026: Executive Order "Continuing the Suspension of Duty-Free De Minimis Treatment for All Countries" (EO 14388) keeps the suspension in force and sets postal duty tied to the Feb 20 2026 surcharge proclamation's rate, assessed on value, until the surcharge expires or CBP's new postal entry process takes effect; country of origin and value must be declared — [White House, Feb 2026 EO](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/02/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries/); [Federal Register, Apr 9 2026 (R1-2026-03829)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/09/R1-2026-03829/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries)
- June 24 2026: CBP interim final rule "Indefinite Suspension of the De Minimis Exemption for Mail Shipments and New Postal Informal Entry Process" (91 FR 37801) plus a companion rule for all other modes (91 FR 37789); CBP grounds the suspension in its own 19 U.S.C. 1321 authority, independent of the EOs; modification or revocation would be announced in the Federal Register — [Federal Register 2026-12669](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry); [CBP trade-related FR notices 2026](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/rulings/cbp-trade-related-federal-register-notices-2026)
- CBP E-Commerce FAQ (last modified Sept 2 2026): suspension effective on FR publication for both postal and non-postal; shipments ≤$800 "no longer qualify for duty-free de minimis treatment"; new mail informal entry process effective July 24 2026; from Oct 22 2026 mail informal entry excludes goods claiming Chapter 98/99 or FTA treatment or subject to PGA requirements; duties follow HTSUS classification, origin and value (no flat fee stated); only the owner/purchaser or a licensed broker may make entry; informal entry ≤$2,500, formal above; a basic importation bond must be on file in ACE eBond for postal entry filers; mail is MPF-exempt except Inbound EMS; gift exemption excludes purchased articles — [CBP E-Commerce FAQs](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/e-commerce/faqs)
- Section 122 surcharge: Proclamation 11012 (Feb 20 2026, same day the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs) imposed an additional 10% ad valorem duty on all countries for 150 days, effective Feb 24 2026 through July 24 2026, stacking on MFN and Section 301 (e.g., a 25% Section 301 Chinese product paid 25% + 10% + MFN); a threatened increase to 15% was never implemented per most sources (Global Trade Alert is the outlier saying it was raised Feb 22); the Court of International Trade held 2–1 the invocation did not meet statutory criteria (ruling on appeal) — [White House: Temporary Import Surcharge proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/02/imposing-a-temporary-import-surcharge-to-address-fundamental-international-payments-problems/); [Federal Register 2026-03824](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03824/imposing-a-temporary-import-surcharge-to-address-fundamental-international-payments-problems); [Gibson Dunn on CIT ruling](https://www.gibsondunn.com/section-122-global-tariffs-invalidated-by-the-court-of-international-trade-ruling-and-next-steps/); [Skadden on CIT ruling (May 2026)](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs); [Global Trade Alert S122 report](https://globaltradealert.org/reports/S122-US-Tariff-Estimates)
- Post-July 24 2026 structure: one vendor source states Section 122 "was replaced by the two-tier 10%/12.5% Section 301 forced-labor tariff" and another says most non-exempt Chinese goods face "a new 12.5% Section 301 tariff" — single-source, unverified against USTR/Federal Register — [TariffsTool Section 122 rates 2026](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/section-122-tariff-rates-2026); [DoDropshipping: dropshipping tariffs](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-tariffs/)
- Who pays: under DDU the buyer handles and pays import duties, taxes and clearance; under DDP the seller covers them; AliExpress often does not collect at checkout, so many US buyers "receive a duty/brokerage bill from the carrier (UPS/FedEx/DHL) on delivery" — [DoDropshipping: dropshipping tariffs](https://dodropshipping.com/dropshipping-tariffs/); [Fulfillbot: AliExpress tariffs 2026](https://fulfillbot.com/?p=1000518)
- CJdropshipping (Apr 2025 blog): shipping fees "now include those tariff charges directly in the freight rate"; goods in CJ's US warehouses have already cleared customs and tariffs — [CJdropshipping blog: adapting to US tariffs](https://blog.cjdropshipping.cn/detail/How-CJdropshipping-Helps-You-Adapt-to-New-U-S--Tariffs)
- Supplier DROIX suspended DDP to the US because "customers could unfortunately still be required to pay the difference in tariffs upon their package entering the USA" — [DROIX shipping announcement](https://droix.net/blogs/important-update-regarding-shipping-to-the-usa/)
- Sellers are adapting by bulk-importing into US warehouses or using DDP so duty is paid once or built into landed price — [China-Fulfillment 2026 survival guide](https://www.china-fulfillment.com/us-de-minimis-ended-2026-china-sellers-guide.html); [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/us-de-minimis-exemption-suspended-2026/)

### Inferences
- Importer of record: under CBP rules only the owner/purchaser (the US consumer, for a direct-ship DDU parcel) or a licensed broker may make entry; in practice the express carrier's broker enters the goods and bills the consignee unless the shipper has prepaid (DDP). For a low-involvement, low-chargeback store, the only acceptable design is DDP (duties built into supplier freight) or US-warehouse fulfillment — a surprise carrier duty bill at the door is a direct chargeback/refund generator and a likely "unfair/deceptive" exposure if not disclosed.
- Because the tariff stack has changed at least three times in 2026 (IEEPA → Section 122 → post-July regime) and is under litigation, landed cost per SKU should be re-quoted from the supplier within 2 weeks of launch and re-checked monthly; price with a buffer.
- Store policy pack (all can be drafted by an assistant, reviewed by the owner): shipping policy with a realistic, supplier-verified delivery window (state a specific range; do not leave it blank, which defaults the FTC clock to 30 days); refund/return policy consistent with the FTC refund-within-7-working-days rule for unshipped orders; privacy policy that matches actual pixels (Meta/TikTok/GA) with a consent banner (Shopify's built-in Customer Privacy / cookie banner settings); terms of service; contact page with a working email and a US address. A tiny store will be below CCPA revenue thresholds but the 100,000-consumer test can be met via traffic, and CIPA exposure exists regardless of size — so consent-before-tracking is the cheap insurance.
- Operational automation that satisfies 16 CFR 435: Shopify Flow (or equivalent) rule — if an order is unfulfilled or untracked after N days (set below the promised window), send the delay notice with a one-click cancel link; if the customer cancels or does not respond when required, auto-refund within 7 working days.

### Gaps
- Exact current duty rate on a typical China-origin dropship SKU (as of Oct 2026) could not be verified from an official source; the "10%/12.5% Section 301" structure post-July 24 2026 is single-source. The report should direct the owner to re-check USTR/CBP at launch.
- Whether the Section 122 duties collected Feb–Jul 2026 are refundable (CIT ruling under appeal) is unresolved; irrelevant to a store launching in November but relevant to supplier pricing.
- I found no CBP page that states in plain terms who the "importer of record" is on a DDU express parcel to a consumer; the inference above relies on CBP's "owner or purchaser" entry rule plus industry sources.
- Shopify's built-in policy generator contents were not reviewed in this pass.

## Key Question 6: Banking and accounting basics — business bank account, bookkeeping, sales tax filing tooling

### Takeaway
Shopify Payments requires an ACH-eligible USD checking account at a US bank (or Shopify Balance); open a dedicated business checking account in the LLC's name with the EIN before onboarding payments. For bookkeeping, QuickBooks Online (~$38/mo Simple Start in 2026, promos often 50% off for 3 months) plus a Shopify connector (A2X ~$29/mo, or Shopify's own free QuickBooks connector for Shopify-Payments-only stores) covers automated reconciliation; Shopify Tax (free under $100k) calculates sales tax and the owner files in Idaho TAP quarterly/monthly as assigned.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify payouts require a full USD checking account at a US bank, ACH-eligible; savings, wire-only, multi-currency and bank-imitating fintech accounts are not supported; Shopify Balance is an alternative — [Shopify Help: US requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/united-states/requirements)
- QuickBooks Online 2026 list prices (one comparison): Simple Start $38, Essentials $75, Plus $115, Advanced $275 per month; an entry Solopreneur/Lite tier around $20; frequent "half price for first three months" promos — [Fit Small Business QBO comparison](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/quickbooks-online-comparison); [Tech.co QBO pricing](https://tech.co/accounting-software/quickbooks-online-pricing)

- QuickBooks Online app on Shopify App Store (OneSaaS): 4.8 rating, 3,027 reviews; handles fees and refunds, though one reviewer reported a 3-week disconnect — [Shopify App Store: QuickBooks Online (OneSaaS)](https://apps.shopify.com/onesaas)

Expect ~$40–70/month in accounting tooling after promos.

## Key Question 7: What strictly requires the human owner vs. what can be delegated to an AI agent/assistant

### Takeaway
Identity-bound steps — SOS filing signature, IRS EIN application as responsible party, bank KYC, Shopify Payments verification (ID photo, SSN, EIN letter, bank link, two-step auth), accepting Shopify/processor terms, and filing/paying taxes under the owner's TAP login — must be done by the owner personally. Everything else (store build, policy drafting, product/listing ops, supplier comms, Flow automations, bookkeeping sync, dispute evidence drafting, monitoring reserves/chargeback ratios) can be delegated via Shopify staff/collaborator access and APIs.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify Payments: for sole props/single-member LLCs "the owner is designated as the account representative"; the representative must be able to "legally bind the company to Shopify Payments Terms of Service"; identity documents must be original camera photos; photo ID may be requested if identity can't be verified automatically; EIN can't be edited after setup without Support — [Shopify Help: US requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/united-states/requirements); [Shopify Help: Setting up Shopify Payments](https://help.shopify.com/manual/payment-settings/shopify-payments/fraud-prevention/)
- Two-step authentication on the Shopify account is required to receive payouts — [Shopify Help: Setting up Shopify Payments](https://help.shopify.com/manual/payment-settings/shopify-payments/fraud-prevention/)
- IRS EIN online: applicant must be the responsible party or an authorized representative and supply the responsible party's SSN/ITIN; one session, 15-minute timeout — [IRS: Apply for an EIN online](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
- Idaho SOS: Certificate of Organization is filed via SOSBiz and must list the registered agent; the organizer signs — [Idaho SOS LLC Certificate of Organization form](https://sos.idaho.gov/CORP/forms/LLC/LLC%20Cert%20org.pdf)
- Idaho seller's permit is registered online at tax.idaho.gov/ibr and filings/payments go through the owner's TAP account — [Idaho STC: Online Sellers Take These Steps](https://tax.idaho.gov/?p=165100)
- Shopify Basic includes staff/collaborator access and the Sidekick assistant; Grow adds up to 5 staff accounts — [Shopify Pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)
- Shopify's Admin GraphQL API (version 2026-10), Shopify CLI with Admin API/bulk operations, and Flow provide programmatic control of products, orders, fulfillments and automations — [shopify.dev changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog); [2026-10 release notes](https://shopify.dev/changelog/release-notes/2026-10)

### Inferences
- Owner-only checklist (one sitting of ~2–3 hours spread over ~1 week, ideally completed by late October 2026): (1) file LLC on SOSBiz and pay $100; (2) get EIN (immediate) and download CP575; (3) open business checking (bring CP575, ID, Certificate of Organization); (4) create Shopify account with owner email, enable two-step auth; (5) complete Shopify Payments verification (SSN, ID photo, EIN letter, bank ACH details, address proof); (6) register Idaho seller's permit at tax.idaho.gov/ibr, then TAP once the permit arrives; (7) accept Shopify ToS, Payments ToS, any supplier/app agreements; (8) each filing period, log into TAP to file/pay (or formally authorize a bookkeeper).
- Delegable to an agent/assistant (with a staff account scoped to the minimum permissions and no access to Payments/bank settings): theme setup, product import and copy, pricing with landed-cost buffers, policy pages, shipping settings, Shopify Tax configuration, Flow rules for FTC delay notices/auto-refunds, supplier order routing (via supplier apps/APIs), customer-service drafts, chargeback evidence packages (owner submits), daily monitoring of refund/dispute ratios and reserve emails, bookkeeping categorization in QBO.
- Hard boundary to respect: KYC documents, SSN, bank credentials and tax-account logins should never be handed to an automated agent; Shopify's rule that the account representative must be an owner able to bind the company makes this a compliance requirement, not just a security preference.

### Gaps
- Whether Idaho TAP supports delegated third-party filing access for a non-CPA assistant was not verified.
- Shopify's current (2026) staff-permission granularity for Payments settings was not checked; assume Payments/bank settings are owner-only.
