# Supplier and Fulfillment Models for a US Dropshipping Store (state of play as of October 8, 2026)

Scope note: all figures are dated inline. "Official" = vendor/government page fetched directly; "third-party" = blog, review aggregator or law-firm summary. Most dropshipping-vendor blogs have a commercial stake (they sell the alternative they recommend); this is flagged where relevant.

## Key Question 1: How did the end of de minimis (Aug 29, 2025) and 2025–2026 tariffs change China-direct dropshipping?

### Takeaway
China-direct parcel dropshipping (AliExpress/CJ China-line/DSers/AutoDS) is now structurally dutiable on every order: de minimis is gone for all countries (by executive order since Aug 29, 2025, by CBP regulation since June 24, 2026 for non-postal and July 24, 2026 for postal, and by statute from July 1, 2027), the IEEPA tariff stack was struck down in Feb 2026 but was replaced by a 10% Section 122 surcharge and then (from July 24, 2026) a new 12.5% Section 301 duty on China/HK/Vietnam that stacks on MFN and legacy 2018 Section 301 rates. Duty collection on AliExpress-style orders is inconsistent (some prepaid at checkout, many billed by the carrier on delivery with brokerage fees), and no measured 2026 data shows China-line delivery times have improved from the historical 7–20 day range; the best available vendor figures still cite 8–15 business days plus longer customs processing.

**Who pays and how duties are collected (DDP / DDU / carrier fees)**

Carriers "may also add administrative fees" — [Shippo (Aug 27, 2025, updated Sept 11, 2025)](https://goshippo.com/blog/us-de-minimis-exemption-ending-what-brands-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- The effective China-origin duty stack for a typical consumer good as of Oct 2026 is MFN (often 0–20%) + legacy 2018 Section 301 (7.5–25% if the HTS code is on a list) + 12.5% new Section 301, i.e. roughly 12.5%–55%+ of customs value, plus per-parcel entry/brokerage costs that are fixed and therefore punishing on sub-$20 items. The IEEPA 54%/120% headline rates of 2025 are gone, so the stack is lower than at its 2025 peak but higher than the 2018–April 2025 duty-free baseline.
- Because postal shipments now require a bonded filer and monthly entry filings (July 24, 2026 rules), China Post/ePacket-style cheap postal lines are effectively unusable for small sellers unless the logistics provider acts as filer; expect China-line fulfillment to migrate entirely to express consolidators (Cainiao, YunExpress, 4PX) operating DDP, with that cost embedded in the per-order shipping fee.
- No app (DSers, AutoDS, CJ, Zendrop) was found to compute or guarantee duty prepayment; the practical risk for a hands-off operator is a customer being billed by a carrier post-delivery, which triggers chargebacks. This makes China-direct the highest-variance model for customer-service load.
- "7–20 days" remains the realistic planning assumption for China-line delivery in 2026; vendor claims of 8–15 business days are 12–21 calendar days, and 2026 reviews report month-plus outliers.

### Gaps
- I found no official CBP or carrier rate card for 2026 UPS/FedEx/DHL entry-preparation fees on US low-value imports; figures above are 2025 or unspecified.
- I found no 2026 measured dataset of China-to-US dropship delivery times (all figures are vendor claims or anecdotes).
- No source states whether AliExpress Choice orders to the US are universally DDP; the "incl. import charges" label is per-listing.
- A claim that "at least 88 national postal operators suspended US-bound parcels" in Sept 2025 appeared in search results but I could not attribute it to a specific verified source.
- No 2026 Marketplace Pulse figure for the share of Temu US sales shipped from US warehouses was found (only the 2024 ~20% figure).
- Primary Reddit threads from 2026 could not be retrieved (search returned only secondhand blog summaries).
- The current CBP postal entry process's status after the Oct 22, 2026 delayed-compliance date is two weeks out and unconfirmed.

## Key Question 2: US-warehouse dropshipping — suppliers, shipping times, costs, margins, MOQs, Shopify integration

### Takeaway
US-warehoused dropshipping (Zendrop US products, CJ US warehouse, Spocket US/EU suppliers, Syncee, Doba, Inventory Source, Wholesale2B) delivers in roughly 2–8 days with no per-order customs exposure, at the cost of $0–$100/month subscriptions, higher unit costs, and a much smaller catalog; the three biggest aggregators show sharply different 2026 reputations (CJ 4.9★ on Shopify but Trustpilot score suspended; Zendrop 4.5★ with recurring billing/fulfillment complaints; Spocket 3.5★ on Shopify and 2.6★ on Capterra with billing-dispute complaints as recent as Sept 2026).

### Inferences
- The practical US-warehouse options for a hands-off Shopify seller reduce to three aggregator apps (Zendrop, CJ, Spocket) plus directory tools (Syncee/Doba/Inventory Source) that require more manual supplier onboarding. Of the three apps, CJ is cheapest (no subscription) but weakest on consistency; Zendrop has the most Shopify-native automation but a documented billing-complaint pattern; Spocket has the fastest advertised US delivery but the worst 2026 review trajectory.
- The "US warehouse" label is product-specific, not platform-wide: Zendrop's US stock covers bestsellers only and CJ's catalog is mostly China-stocked. A seller must filter to US-stocked SKUs and verify with a test order; otherwise the China-line economics of Question 1 apply.
- MOQs are effectively zero on all three apps (true per-order dropship), but US-stocked unit costs are higher and selection is thin, so realistic net margins after ads are in the 5–15% band cited above unless the product is differentiated.

## Key Question 3: Print-on-demand (Printful, Printify, Gelato, Gooten, Apliiq) — pricing, US production times, margins, risk

### Takeaway
POD is the lowest-operational-risk physical model for a hands-off US seller: zero inventory, US-based production (Printful says 86% of orders are fulfilled in the customer's region), 1–5 business-day production plus 3–7 day domestic shipping, free-tier platforms with 4.8★ Shopify ratings, and fully automated order routing/tracking. The trade-off is lower absolute margin on commodity apparel (roughly $8–$10 per $25 tee before ads) and dependence on design/marketing rather than sourcing.

## Key Question 4: Branded/private-label "dropship from a 3PL" (small inventory + 3PL or Amazon MCF) — capital, costs, when it beats pure dropshipping

### Takeaway
Holding a small inventory in a US 3PL or Amazon MCF gives 2–3 day delivery, full branding control, and one documented customs entry instead of per-parcel duties, but it reintroduces capital risk and fixed fees: ShipBob-class 3PLs run ~$275/month minimums and are uneconomic below ~400 orders/month per one CFO-advisory analysis, while Amazon MCF charges roughly $6.50–$10.60 per single-unit order (plus a 3.5% surcharge and peak fees Oct 15, 2026–Jan 14, 2027) with no monthly minimum, making MCF the lower-commitment entry point. It beats pure dropshipping once a product is validated and repeat volume justifies a bulk buy.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon MCF official page (fetched Oct 2026): 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge on US MCF fees; holiday peak fees Oct 15, 2026–Jan 14, 2027; remote-area surcharge +100% (standard) / +200% (oversize) outside the contiguous 48; 2026 MCF fees rose an average $0.30/unit, unchanged for 3+ unit orders in small/large standard; discounts: up to 50% off multi-unit orders, 25% off first 100 units for new MCF sellers, Preferred Pricing up to 15% off plus up to $1 FBA credit per unit for up to 12 months, TikTok Shop 35% off for 12 months (listed July 16, 2026); storage fees per Seller Central; rate card at supplychain.amazon.com/docs/rate-card — [Amazon Supply Chain MCF pricing](https://supplychain.amazon.com/mcf/pricing)
- Third-party MCF rate samples: Jan 15, 2026 increase of $0.35–$0.41 per single unit — [AMZ Prep MCF fees 2026](https://amzprep.com/?p=16673); peak card: small standard ≤4 oz $7.18 standard (3-day) / $10.38 expedited (2-day); large standard 1–2 lb $10.60 / $13.37 — [My Amazon Guy (2026)](https://myamazonguy.com/fba/amazon-mcf-for-shopify-and-tiktok-shop/); small standard <1 lb ~$6.59 at standard speed — [Threecolts](https://www.threecolts.com/blog/how-amazon-mcf-powers-fulfillment-across-every-marketplace-you-sell-on/); Preferred Pricing tiers recalculated weekly on 12-week rolling volume, top 15% tier at ~19,000 units/12 weeks — [Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-mcf-preferred-pricing-2026)
- ShipBob: Growth plan for 0–400 orders/month; third-party figures: $275 monthly minimum; setup ~$975 (sometimes waived) or free self-service onboarding under 400 orders/month; storage ~$10/shelf, $5/bin, $40/pallet per month (one source $40–80/pallet after 30-day grace); receiving $25/hr for first two hours then $40/hr; postage markup 15–30%; 3–5% Q4 storage surcharges and a 3% dashboard fee on some 2026 quotes; typical all-in $5–$10 per order; "sweet spot is 400 to 500 plus orders per month: below that, setup fees and monthly minimums make the per-order math punitive" — [ShipBob startup page](https://www.shipbob.com/?p=6077); [Ecommerce-Platforms ShipBob pricing](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/articles/shipbob-pricing); [Eightx 3PL cost analysis (2026)](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-3pl-pick-pack-cost-by-order-size-2026); [AuthenCIO hidden fees](https://www.authencio.com/blog/shipbob-pricing-hidden-fees-6-top-3pl-alternatives)
- ShipMonk cited with a $250/month minimum and lower setup fee — [Eightx ShipBob vs ShipMonk](https://eightx.co/blog/shipbob-shipmonk-regional-3pl-cost)
- Bulk import + domestic fulfillment "concentrates duty payments into documented shipments"; landed-cost formula = product cost + international freight + duties + broker fees + domestic freight + FBA fees — [Seller Labs (Aug 1, 2026)](https://www.sellerlabs.com/knowledge-base/tariffs-duties-and-section-321-whats-changing-in-2026/)
- CJ supports "pre-purchasing inventory" with MOQ guidance (Sept 1, 2026 review) — [Shopify App Store: CJ](https://apps.shopify.com/cucheng); Zendrop custom packaging available on the Plus plan — [Branvas](https://branvas.com/blogs/news/spocket-vs-zendrop)
- Private-label margin benchmark 50%+ vs 15–30% dropshipping (vendor) — [SellerSprite (2026)](https://sellersprite.ai/en/blog/amazon-fba-vs-dropshipping-vs-shopify-2026)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable capital is not published anywhere I found; from the fee structures above, a lean MCF-based private-label test is roughly: one bulk order (e.g., 200–300 units at $5–$10 landed = $1,500–$3,000 including the 12.5%+ duty stack and freight), FBA inbound/storage, and ~$7–$11 MCF fulfillment per order, with no monthly minimum. A ShipBob-class 3PL adds ~$275/month minimum plus setup, so it only makes sense above a few hundred orders/month.
- This model beats pure dropshipping when (a) a SKU has proven demand (so inventory risk is bounded), (b) the seller wants branded unboxing or faster delivery than US-aggregator dropship, and (c) order volume is high enough that per-order duties/brokerage on parcels exceed one consolidated entry.
- For a seller wanting minimal involvement, MCF is more hands-off than a 3PL (no contract negotiation, API integration to Shopify exists), but it requires an Amazon seller account and FBA inbound logistics.

### Gaps
- No source quantified a "minimum viable capital" figure for a 3PL/MCF private-label launch; the estimate above is my inference.
- Amazon's official MCF rate card (supplychain.amazon.com/docs/rate-card) could not be fetched; per-unit rates are third-party.
- ShipBob does not publish a rate card; all figures are secondhand and conflict on pick/pack fees.

## Overall ranking of models for a hands-off US seller targeting profitability by January 2027

### Takeaway
Ranked from least to most risky on the five criteria (risk, margin, shipping time, quality control, automation): (1) Print-on-demand via Printful/Printify; (2) US-warehoused aggregator dropshipping (Zendrop US SKUs / CJ US warehouse; avoid Spocket in 2026); (3) small-inventory private label via Amazon MCF (lower risk than a 3PL at low volume); (4) 3PL-held inventory (ShipBob-class) — only above ~400 orders/month; (5) China-direct AliExpress/CJ-China via DSers/AutoDS — highest customs, delivery-time and refund risk in 2026. Digital products are lower-risk than all five but outside a dropshipping store's core.

### Cited Findings
- Criteria evidence is consolidated from Questions 1–7 above; key anchors: 12.5% new Section 301 on China/HK/Vietnam from July 24, 2026 stacking on MFN and legacy 301 — [Dorsey](https://www.dorsey.com/newsresources/publications/client-alerts/2026/7/announced-section-301-tariffs); per-parcel entry/brokerage estimates $4–$25 — [Fulfillbot](https://fulfillbot.com/?p=1000518); US-warehouse delivery 2–7 business days — [Ecommerce-Platforms](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/articles/zendrop-vs-cjdropshipping); Printful 86% same-region fulfillment — [Printful](https://www.printful.com/pricing); MCF ~$6.59–$10.60 per single unit plus 3.5% surcharge — [Amazon](https://supplychain.amazon.com/mcf/pricing), [Threecolts](https://www.threecolts.com/blog/how-amazon-mcf-powers-fulfillment-across-every-marketplace-you-sell-on/); ShipBob economics punitive below ~400 orders/month — [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-3pl-pick-pack-cost-by-order-size-2026); Spocket 3.5★/2.6★ 2026 ratings — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/spocket), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205029/Spocket/reviews/)

### Inferences
- Scorecard (my synthesis; H = favorable):
  - POD (Printful/Printify): risk H (no inventory, no customs), margin M (~$8–10/tee before ads; 40–60% gross anecdotal), shipping H (1–5 day production + 3–7 day US ship), QC H for Printful/M for Printify, automation H.
  - US-warehouse aggregator (Zendrop/CJ US): risk M-H (no inventory, but supplier-fault refund rules, limited catalog, billing complaints), margin L-M (5–15% net benchmark), shipping H (2–8 days), QC M, automation H for routing/tracking, M for refunds.
  - Private label via MCF: risk M (capital at risk, bounded by one small bulk buy), margin H (50%+ gross benchmark), shipping H (2–3 day), QC H (you inspect the lot), automation M-H.
  - 3PL inventory: as MCF but with $250–$275/month minimums and setup fees; risk M-L below 400 orders/month.
  - China-direct (DSers/AutoDS/CJ China line): risk L (duty stack, inconsistent DDP, carrier billing, chargebacks, month-long outliers), margin L-M (10-point margin hit in the Inventory Source example), shipping L (8–15 business days nominal), QC L, automation H for routing but L for exceptions.
- Lowest-risk path to profitability by January 2027 with minimal involvement: launch POD (free tier) and/or US-stocked Zendrop/CJ SKUs validated with test orders in October–November 2026, then graduate winning SKUs to an MCF bulk buy in Q1 2027 once demand is proven. Avoid China-line parcels for US customers during the Oct 15, 2026–Jan 14, 2027 peak window and the Oct 22, 2026 postal compliance change.

### Gaps
- The ranking relies on vendor and law-firm sources rather than measured delivery or refund-rate data; no independent 2026 benchmark comparing these models side by side was found.
