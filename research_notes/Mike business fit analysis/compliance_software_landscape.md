# Food-Safety / QMS / Connected-Worker Software Landscape for Small-to-Mid US F&B Plants (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: US market, plants of ~20-500 employees. Research date 2026-10-08. Most pricing is quote-based and therefore comes from third-party aggregators (Capterra, G2, SelectHub, ITQlick, rfp.wiki, vendor-authored comparison pages). Where a vendor's own competitor page is the source (QTRACA, FoodDocs, Allera, FoodReady, SafetyCulture blogs all publish "best of" lists ranking themselves first), that is flagged. No Reddit threads surfaced in any search despite several attempts; the closest "real user" forum evidence is IFSQN (International Food Safety & Quality Network), which is the de-facto QA-manager forum for this industry.

## Key Question 1: Who are the main vendors in (a) food safety/QMS, (b) connected worker / digital work instructions / checklists, (c) AI SOP / document generation?

### Takeaway
Category (a) is crowded with ~20+ near-identical "digital forms + HACCP + document control + supplier docs" vendors spanning from $60/location/month restaurant tools to $10k+/month enterprise platforms; category (b) has consolidated hard (Redzone->QAD, Poka->IFS, Parsable->CAI, SwipeGuide->L2L, Acadia->Epicor, MaintainX->Autodesk) and is priced for 300+ person plants; category (c) is young, with FoodReady, IONI, PinkPepper, Allera and SystemPath Beacon shipping "AI HACCP/SOP generators" in 2025-26 and every incumbent bolting on an AI assistant.

### Inferences
- The food-safety QMS category splits into three tiers: (1) restaurant/foodservice checklist apps ($60-$300/location/month: Zip HACCP, FoodDocs, Squadle, Mapal, ComplianceMate, Jolt, Operandio, Xenia) that reviewers explicitly say are not full manufacturing compliance programs; (2) a thin SMB-manufacturing middle ($200-$600/month: QTRACA, Smart Food Safe, IONI, Tracktile, Allera, FoodReady-without-consulting) populated mostly by sub-$2M-funded startups and non-US-founded companies; (3) enterprise/mid-market platforms ($1,000-$10,000+/month plus $5k-$50k implementation: SafetyChain, Safefood 360, TraceGains, Trustwell, QT9, ETQ, Intelex, Veeva).
- Connected-worker vendors have almost all been absorbed into ERP/EAM/CAD parents (QAD, IFS, CAI, Epicor, L2L, Autodesk), which pushes their go-to-market further up-market and away from the 50-150 person plant.
- AI SOP/HACCP generation is now table stakes marketing for new entrants; the differentiator being claimed is human/expert review and regulatory grounding, not the generation itself.

## Key Question 2: What do they charge, what plant size do they target, and which are too expensive/heavy for a 50-150 person plant?

### Takeaway
Enterprise FSQA platforms run $1k-$10k+/month plus $2k-$50k implementation and are explicitly built for multi-site 300+ person operations; the SMB-native tier is $200-$600/month with implementation included; connected-worker platforms (Redzone, Poka, Parsable, Augmentir, Tulip) are quote-only, roughly $100-$150/user/month when a number is visible, and are consistently described as 2-month implementations with change-management budgets, i.e., too heavy for a single 50-150 person plant without IT staff.

### Cited Findings
- SafetyChain is billed monthly per facility with no per-user fees; third-party estimates: SelectHub "starting $1,000+"; ITQlick "starts at $10,000/year for small organizations," "$8,000-$25,000/month for a 100-user enterprise," plus "$5,000-$50,000+" implementation/customization/training/migration — [SafetyChain pricing FAQ](https://safetychain.com/pricing); [SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/quality-management-software/safetychain/); [ITQlick](https://www.itqlick.com/safetychain). A comparison site says SafetyChain is "best for large, multi-facility operations" and that "operations moving from paper to digital for the first time will likely find the learning curve steep" — [Smart Food Safe roundup](https://smartfoodsafe.com/best-haccp-compliance-software/).
- QTRACA's (vendor-authored but numerically specific) pricing guide gives market bands: transparent subscription $80-$500/mo; tiered $200-$2,000+/mo; quote-based $1,000-$10,000+/mo; consulting-bundled $1,500-$5,000/mo. Hidden costs: implementation $2,000-$10,000 at enterprise platforms, IoT sensors $30-$50/sensor/month at some vendors, per-user fees $15-$30/user/mo, custom integrations $5,000-$25,000, multi-site add-ons. 5-year single-site TCO (15 users, 8 sensors): $21,840 (QTRACA) vs $42k-$72k (mid-tier) vs $95k-$185k (quote-based sold downmarket) vs $120k-$240k (consulting bundle) — [QTRACA pricing guide](https://www.qtraca.com/food-safety-software-pricing-guide).

## Key Question 4: What do small/mid plants actually use today? Evidence on paper/Excel/binders

## Key Question 7: Which categories are oversaturated and which have thin coverage?

### Takeaway
Oversaturated: generic digital checklists/inspection apps (dozens, now led by an 80,000-org Mitti), restaurant/foodservice HACCP apps, enterprise multi-site FSQA, supplier-document/traceability networks, CMMS, and AI "HACCP plan generators" (at least five launched 2025-26); thin: a plant-floor FSQA + SOP system priced and implementable for a single 50-150 person plant without IT, dairy/PMO/aseptic-specific record workflows, audit-evidence export/binder generation, and anything combining regulatory domain depth with the new AI document tooling.

### Inferences
- The crowded layers are horizontal (any checklist, any HACCP plan); the thin layer is vertical depth: PMO/aseptic/SQF-edition-specific workflows, pre-built record sets that match what state dairy inspectors and SQF/BRC auditors actually ask for, and audit-day evidence packaging. A single founder cannot out-build Mitti on forms, but can out-know it on dairy.
- Pricing white space: per-site unlimited-user at ~$300-$800/month with implementation included and no IT dependency sits between Zip/FoodDocs (too restaurant-shaped) and SafetyChain (too heavy); QTRACA and Smart Food Safe are the only identified occupants and both are non-US-founded with thin US presence.
- AI document generation alone is already commoditized; defensibility would come from the regulatory corpus (PMO, 21 CFR 113/117, SQF code), validated templates, and workflow around review/approval/Part 11 signatures rather than generation.
