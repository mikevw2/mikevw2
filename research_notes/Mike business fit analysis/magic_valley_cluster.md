# Magic Valley / Southern Idaho Food & Dairy Processing Cluster — Local Market Notes

Research date: 2026-10-08. Older data is flagged with its year. Employee counts are the most recent figure found; many are 2018–2024 and should be treated as estimates. Job-posting data was pulled from the Indeed and ZipRecruiter MCP tools on 2026-10-08 (postings dated Feb–Oct 2026).

## KQ1. Every significant food/beverage/dairy facility within ~60 miles of Twin Falls (company, location, product, headcount, ownership)

### Takeaway
Within ~60 miles of Twin Falls there are roughly 20 dairy plants plus ~10 other sizeable food plants, anchored by five "mega" sites (Chobani ~1,100–1,200; Glanbia ~900 across Idaho; McCain Burley ~730+ pre-expansion; Lamb Weston Twin Falls ~650 in 2018; Agropur Jerome ~400) and a long tail of 30–250-employee independents. The cluster is still adding capacity (Chobani $500M expansion operational ~2026, Idaho Milk Products $190M ice-cream/blending plant opened May 2026, Suntado aseptic plant opened 2024, Darigold Jerome sold to Actus June 2026), with one closure (Idahoan Rupert, June 2026).

## KQ2. Small/mid independents vs. large multinationals with corporate IT / enterprise QMS

### Takeaway
By headcount the cluster is dominated by corporate-owned plants (Chobani, Glanbia plc, Agropur co-op, McCain, Lamb Weston, Mondelēz, Amalgamated, Gossner, Actus/Butterfly Equity), but there is a real middle tier of locally owned, 35–250-employee processors (Idaho Milk Products, High Desert Milk, Suntado, MVQMP, Commercial Creamery, Rite Stuff, Mart Group, Brewster Rupert, Riverence, Rangen, Independent Meat) and a micro tier (Ballard, Cloverleaf, Evans, breweries) that are far more likely to lack enterprise QMS.

### Cited Findings
- Large/corporate (parent with corporate IT): Chobani (private, NY HQ), Glanbia plc, Agropur (QC co-op), McCain (Canada), Lamb Weston (NYSE), Mondelēz/Clif, Amalgamated Sugar (Snake River Sugar co-op), Gossner (UT family, ~550–600 staff), Actus Nutrition (Butterfly Equity), Darigold Boise (NDA co-op), Lactalis Nampa — ownership per [ISDA Guide 2023](https://agri.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/20230401-A-Guide-to-Idahos-Dairy-Processors-online.pdf), [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/industry-news-developments/2026/06/03/actus-nutrition-darigold-finalize-strategic-partnership-for-milk-proteins-other-specialty-ingredients/), [GrowinCo Feb 2026](https://www.growinco.com/blog/top-10-dairy-co-manufacturers-idaho).
- Locally owned mid-tier: Idaho Milk Products (3 dairy families; now two plants), High Desert Milk (6 dairymen co-op), Suntado (two dairy families), MVQMP (producer co-op, 35 staff 2018), Mart Group (Rupert family co.), Brewster Rupert (80), Commercial Creamery (Spokane family co.), Rite Stuff (private), Riverence/Idaho Trout Processors, Rangen, Independent Meat — sources as cited in KQ1.

### Inferences
- Roughly 10–12 corporate sites vs. 10–12 local mid-tier vs. 5–8 micro sites. The mid-tier (especially IMP's new ice-cream plant, Suntado's phased build-out, High Desert, MVQMP, Commercial Creamery, Mart Frozen Foods, Rite Stuff) is the realistic bootstrap target: SQF/BRC-certified, growing, locally decided, and showing no named QMS platform in postings.
- Even corporate sites show paper/Excel-era language ("logbooks," "Appendix N documentation," "documenting everything," "paperwork") at the floor level, suggesting plant-level pain points that corporate QMS does not fully solve.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence (vendor case studies, RFPs) of which QMS/SPC platforms any Magic Valley plant runs; only indirect signals from job postings.

## KQ3. Idaho dairy industry stats (plant counts, milk rank, certified sites)

### Takeaway
Idaho is the #3 U.S. milk state (18.26B lb in 2025, ~350 dairies, ~668K cows) with roughly 19–22 dairy processing plants statewide, most of them in the Magic Valley; at least 9 Magic Valley dairy sites are documented as SQF- or BRC-certified, but no state-level GFSI count could be retrieved because the SQFI directory is JavaScript-gated.

### Cited Findings
- Idaho produced 18.26B lb milk from 350 dairy operations in 2025, reclaiming #3 from Texas (18.21B); U.S. total 232B lb — [Idaho Farm Bureau](https://www.idahofb.org/news-room/posts/idaho-bumps-texas-to-reclaim-no-3-milk-state/); [Dairy Herd](https://www.dairyherd.com/idaho-overtakes-texas-reclaim-no-3-spot-u-s-milk-production). January 2026 Idaho output +3.2% YoY on 22,000 more cows — [Idaho Business Review Mar 2026](https://idahobusinessreview.com/2026/03/06/idaho-third-us-milk-production-2025/).
- 667,981 cows, >350 family dairies, 33,000+ jobs, 16B lb/yr (2023) — [ISDA Guide 2023](https://agri.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/20230401-A-Guide-to-Idahos-Dairy-Processors-online.pdf).
- Plant counts: "19 dairy processing facilities" statewide, centered in Magic Valley — [Post Register](https://www.postregister.com/idahos-dairy-industry-a-powerhouse-that-keeps-on-growing/article_e21522ea-0f7c-4ca4-8b77-481ca096be9d.html); "20 dairy processing plants" and no new plant between Chobani (2012) and 2019 — [Idaho Farm Bureau 2019](https://www.idahofb.org/news-room/posts/idaho-dairy-industry-lands-a-new-milk-processing-plant/); "22 dairy processing plants" cited in 2023 — [Hoard's](https://hoards.com/article-33701-new-state-of-the-art-dairy-processing-plant-in-idahos-magic-valley-to-fill-growing-demand-for-shelf-stable-milk-products.html).
