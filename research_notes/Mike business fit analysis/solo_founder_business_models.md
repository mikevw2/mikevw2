# Business models that work for a solo technical founder / domain expert in food & dairy manufacturing compliance (Twin Falls, ID; employed; no outside capital)

Research date: 2026-10-08. US context; Idaho-specific where law/programs differ. "Anecdote" flags forum posts and single founder stories. Roughly 45 searches/fetches were run; where a primary source could not be reached (BizQuest listings returned 403, Idaho legislature chapter index had no text) it is noted.

## KQ1. Vertical SaaS reality for bootstrappers: time to $10k MRR, SMB ACV, sales cycle, churn, "founder must sell in person"

### Takeaway
Reaching $10k MRR puts a bootstrapped SaaS in roughly the top 5-10% of revenue-generating indie SaaS, and the median path is 12-36 months from first paying customer; SMB manufacturing ACVs cluster around $4k-$15k (median ~$8k) with 14-60 day cycles once a buyer is engaged, but SMB churn (25-50%/yr by one dataset; 5.7%/mo median gross MRR churn at $25-100 ARPA per ChartMogul) is the structural enemy, and the manufacturing founders who made it (Limble, Vegam) describe multi-year, side-job, one-plant-at-a-time starts rather than self-serve growth.

### Cited Findings
- MicroConf/TinySeed 2024 State of Independent SaaS (just under 700 responses): average MoM growth ~17%; SMB-targeting companies grow ~12% MoM avg vs enterprise ~26%; free-trial-with-card has highest growth (14% MoM) and ~5.5% MoM churn; freemium ~11% MoM churn; no-card trial has highest LTV (~$6.5k); ~70% of mostly-bootstrapped SaaS have one founder (Rob's recollection); 23.5% plan to seek funding — [Startups for the Rest of Us ep. 721](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-721-7-key-takeaways-from-the-2024-state-of-independent-saas-report)
- TrustMRR-based dataset (3,787 bootstrapped SaaS with non-zero revenue, vendor dataset): median MRR $145; 90th percentile $5,107; 95th percentile $13,205; average $4,298 skewed by outliers; 44% of revenue-generating startups under $100 MRR — [BigIdeasDB State of Indie SaaS Revenue 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-indie-saas-revenue-2026)
- Median time to $10k MRR for bootstrapped SaaS cited as 12-18 months from first paying customer (second-hand via DEV post from TrustMRR data; methodology unverified) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/insightlab/the-annual-planning-framework-for-solo-saas-founders-3nld); founder anecdotes on Indie Hackers: 1,035 days ($0 to $10k) and "3 years from $0-10k MRR is just about typical" for founders who publish charts; fastest ~6-7 months — [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/heres-how-to-hit-10k-mrr-faster-891549221d)
- SaaS Capital 2026 survey (private B2B SaaS): median bootstrapped SaaS grows 15%/yr; 90th percentile 42.3% — [DEV Community summary](https://dev.to/insightlab/the-founders-guide-to-saas-metrics-that-actually-matter-and-which-to-ignore-340d)
- ChartMogul (aggregated data from 2,500+ SaaS businesses): monthly gross MRR churn for $25-100 ARPA — top decile 2.5%, top quartile 3.7%, median 5.7%; for <$25 ARPA 3.3%/5.4%/8.2%; target <3.5%/mo for ARPA <$100 and <2.5% for ARPA >$100; median monthly logo churn 6.1% under $25 ARPA falling to 2.2% above $500 — [ChartMogul churn cheat sheet](https://chartmogul.com/resources/churn-cheat-sheet); [ChartMogul customer churn](https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/)
- Churnkey: 3-5% monthly B2B churn typical for early-stage; <2% net churn "great" — [Churnkey](https://churnkey.co/blog/what-is-a-good-monthly-churn-rate/)
- B2B SaaS ACV benchmark compilation (marketing agency): B2B Manufacturing SaaS SMB band $4k-$15k ACV (median $8k); mid-market median $52k — [GrowthSpree ACV benchmarks 2026](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-average-deal-size-acv-benchmarks-2026-by-vertical-segment-arr-stage-gtm-motion.md)
- Sales cycle by segment (Optifai study of 939 companies, via vendor blogs): SMB <$15k ACV 14-30 days; mid-market $15k-$100k 30-90 days; enterprise 90-180+ days; overall median B2B SaaS cycle 84 days; cycles lengthened 22% since 2022; SMB annual churn 25-50% vs <10% enterprise — [Optifai ACV glossary](https://optif.ai/glossary/acv/); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/sales-cycle-length); [Komo SMB sales](https://komo.ai/glossary/smb-sales)
- CIENCE research (secondhand): 96% of SMB purchases are made by the CEO acting alone — [Komo](https://komo.ai/glossary/smb-sales)
- Rob Walling, Stair Step: "there's a long ramp to any kind of substantial revenue" for SaaS, which is why he recommends a one-time-sale product first and a single traffic channel; "trying to create something too complex" is the biggest first-timer pitfall; recurring revenue is "the holy grail" but should be step 3 — [MicroConf Stair Step article](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping)
- Walling (ep. 610): a small SaaS at "$5000 a month" could sell for about 5x annual profit today; he would build on an app marketplace (Shopify/HubSpot/Salesforce) for distribution, accepting a 10-30% rev share; expects "nights and weekends" early — [Startups for the Rest of Us ep. 610](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-610-how-i-would-start-over-today-bad-habits-of-solopreneurs-and-the-benefits-of-a-day-job-a-rob-solo-adventure)
- Stripo 2026 compilation: 3.5% median monthly churn for B2B SaaS; 106% median B2B NRR — [Stripo Research](https://research.stripo.email/ssential-saas-business-metrics-2026)

### Inferences
- At an $8k SMB manufacturing ACV, $10k MRR = ~15 plants. At 5.7%/mo gross churn you lose ~half your base yearly unless plants are stickier than typical SMBs (compliance tools likely are, but no vertical-specific churn data was found).
- The 14-30 day "SMB cycle" figures describe time-in-pipeline after a buyer is engaged; they do not include the months of relationship-building plant sales usually require. No data was found specific to plant/QA-manager buyers; the Limble story (2.5 years to first customer) is the better planning anchor for cold-start vertical SaaS in manufacturing.
- Walling's own prescription for someone with a day job is explicitly NOT "start with SaaS": start with a one-time-sale product or a service, get to replacement income, then SaaS.

### Gaps
- No source quantified sales cycle length or close rate when selling to plant managers / QA managers specifically; nor "must sell in person" rates for plant software. Treat as unknown.
- Food-safety / QMS SaaS churn benchmarks not published anywhere found.
- MicroConf report MRR distribution and "percent of founders with day job" not retrieved (report at stateofindiesaas.com not fetched).

## KQ2. Founder stories: software bootstrapped for manufacturing / food safety (FoodDocs, Safefood 360, iAuditor, Limble, Vegam, others)

### Takeaway
Every traceable origin story in this niche is "practitioner/consultant first, software second," took years (Limble 2.5 years to first $12/mo customer, 8 years bootstrapped; Vegam 7 years on one plant before productizing; SafetyCulture 8 years selling documents/DVDs before iAuditor), and the ones that scaled fastest eventually took venture money (FoodDocs 2022, Limble 2023).

### Cited Findings
- FoodDocs: founded 2017 in Tallinn by Katrin Liivat and Karin Repp, "two female HACCP consultants"; Liivat built it because as a consultant she saw how hard compliance was; ~$40k/month revenue and ~20 employees in late 2022 (self-reported); raised ~$2.4M in Feb 2022 (Bonnier Ventures et al.); later claims 15,000+ customers — [FoodDocs About](https://www.fooddocs.com/about); [Starter Story](https://starterstory.com/stories/fooddocs); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/02/10/hungry-for-change-fooddocs-eats-up-ps18-million-to-bring-in-ai-to-solve-food-safety-compliance-complexities/)
- Safefood 360: co-founder George Howlett spent "two decades" as food safety technologist, quality manager, auditor and consultant, and lectured ~20 years on an MSc in Food Safety at a Dublin technical university before founding; moved from CEO to Director of Digital Innovation by 2020 — [Safefood 360 masterclass post](https://safefood360.com/?p=14976); [IFSQN webinar bio](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/calendar/event/467-validation-of-cleaning-programs). (No founding-capital or revenue data found.)
- SafetyCulture/iAuditor: Luke Anear ran workers'-comp surveillance investigations 1997-2004 (~2,500 investigations); founded 2004 "from Luke's garage," originally selling safety compliance documents and DVDs (lower-quality source); iAuditor launched 2011/2012 when smartphones reached construction workers — [Dynamic Business profile](https://dynamicbusiness.com.au/entrepreneur-profile/stay-hungry-stay-humble-safetycultures-luke-anear-on-future-proofing-his-tech-company.html); [SafetyCulture company news](https://safetyculture.com/company-news/growing-global-impact-kansas-city); [Construction Magazine 2016](https://publication.constructiondigital.com/construction-magazine-may-2016/0386931001688465327/p14)
- Limble CMMS: Bryan Christiansen's prior pharmacy-software startup failed from "building before validating demand"; ~200 maintenance-manager interviews; founded 2015, built while working for his father part-time; ~2.5 years to first customer ($12/month); "We spent eight years bootstrapped"; "I wouldn't even have called it a side hustle... I was not [making money]"; ~6 more years to traction; Series B $58M at $450M valuation June 2023 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/22/limble-raises-58m-at-a-450m-valuation-to-help-orgs-track-and-maintain-dumb-physical-assets); [Startup Ignition podcast](https://podcastrex.com/shows/startup-ignition-podcast/bryan-christiansen-bootstrapping-to-millions-in-arr-landing-goldman-sachs-customer-obsession); [yespress aggregator claims ~$2.6-2.7M ARR before first institutional money](https://yespress.io/limble)
- Vegam Solutions (Subramanyam Kasibhat): he and his wife were contracted by a friend to build software for one German-owned plant; "we had no manufacturing background"; worked with that single customer for seven years, then rewrote it as a product; now ~180 employees and nearly $10M revenue with no outside funding, selling to large complex plants — [Practical Founders ep. 71](https://practicalfounders.com/podcast/71-bootstrapped-the-core-software-that-runs-complex-manufacturing-plants-subramanyam-kasibhat/)
- Fulcrum (Sunny Han): started in consulting "working with hundreds of manufacturers," then built cloud manufacturing software; went self-funded to Series A — [Launch Minnesota bio](https://mn.gov/launchmn/about/advisoryboard/yu-summy-han.jsp)
- Walling's own path: consulting nights/weekends, then acquired ~20-25 small online businesses, then Drip — [SFTROU ep. 610](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-610-how-i-would-start-over-today-bad-habits-of-solopreneurs-and-the-benefits-of-a-day-job-a-rob-solo-adventure)

### Inferences
- The common pattern is contract/consulting work for one plant that later becomes a product (Vegam, FoodDocs), not a product launched cold. For this founder, "paid custom build for one plant" is the historically validated entry point.
- Limble's 2.5-years-to-first-customer while employed is the closest analogue to this founder's constraints and is the most sobering data point.

### Gaps
- Dozuki, SwipeGuide, MaintainX, Poka origin stories were not researched (searches prioritized elsewhere); MaintainX and Poka are venture-backed from early on and thus weak comparables.
- No Safefood 360 funding/revenue history found.

## KQ3. The "builder who never sells" failure pattern and the cure (pre-selling, design partners, LOIs, paid pilots)

### Takeaway
"No market need" is the top or second reason in every CB Insights post-mortem tally (35-42%), Limble's founder attributes his first failure to building before validating, and the consistent prescription is a paid or signed commitment (deposit, paid pilot, LOI with price and trigger) from 3-6 design partners before significant build.

### Cited Findings
- CB Insights 2021 (101 post-mortems): "no market need" 42%, "ran out of cash" 29%, "not the right team" 23%; outcompeted 19%; pricing/cost 18%; product without business model 17% — [CMO Council summary](https://cmocouncil.org/expert-views/cmo-blog/define-the-need-before-you-bleed-marketing-rigor-can-reduce-start-up-failure); a later CB Insights update ranks "ran out of cash" 38% and "no market need" 35% — [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/analysis/startups-fail-founders-capital/)
- Limble founder: core mistake of first startup was "building before validating demand"; cure was ~200 customer interviews before committing — [Startup Ignition podcast](https://podcastrex.com/shows/startup-ignition-podcast/bryan-christiansen-bootstrapping-to-millions-in-arr-landing-goldman-sachs-customer-obsession)
- Design-partner guidance: distinguish LOI (non-binding), pilot (tests a mature product), design partnership (shapes product pre-build); aim for ~3-6 committed partners with weekly/biweekly calls; design-partner agreements must include IP assignment or diligence flags risk; LOIs should state pricing, rollout date, deliverables, and trigger — [Mika Reyes, design partnerships](https://mikareyes.com/blog/how-to-run-an-effective-design-partnership); [Common Paper summary](https://echai.ventures/startingup/from/common-paper); [Capwave on LOIs](https://capwave.ai/blog/early-traction-without-revenue-how-to-use-waitlists-pilots-and-lois-to-convince-investors)
- Validation test framing: whether "a specific person with a specific problem will take a specific action, such as a call, a deposit, a signed letter of intent, or a spot on a paid pilot list"; "most people will say an idea sounds interesting regardless of whether they would ever pay" — [FirstSales pre-launch outbound validation](https://firstsales.io/blog/pre-launch-outbound-validation/)
- E-Myth (Gerber): most founders are "technicians suffering from an entrepreneurial seizure" who make the "fatal assumption" that knowing the technical work means knowing how to build a business doing that work — [Tim Ferriss interview with Gerber](https://tim.blog/2008/02/27/escaping-the-entrepreneurial-seizure-interview-with-michael-gerber-plus-tim-speaking/)
- Walling: first product should be an add-on to an existing ecosystem with "built in discovery through the plugin repository or app store"; single traffic source — [MicroConf Stair Step](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping)
- Jonathan Stark: hourly billing creates an "artificial ceiling," strains trust and encourages scope creep; he moved from estimating hours to asking "why" questions to produce value-based proposals — [Being Freelance podcast](https://beingfreelance.com/season-12/freelance-business-coach-jonathan-stark)

### Inferences
- For a founder with multiple unlaunched products, the data points to a rule: no new code until a plant has signed an LOI with a price or paid a deposit for a pilot. The founder's employer relationships are the obvious design-partner pool, subject to the legal constraints in KQ8.
- The "add-on to an existing ecosystem" advice maps onto building inside/alongside tools plants already run (SafetyChain, Redzone, SafetyCulture/Mitti templates, MaintainX), which also overlaps with the partner model in KQ6.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found measuring success rates of pre-selling vs. build-first (the "Demand Validation Playbook" 73%-vs-8% claim is a paid-course marketing figure and was not credited).

## KQ4. Productized consulting in food safety: examples, pricing (retainer / per-audit / per-plan), firm size, client acquisition, local-cluster role, solo income

### Takeaway
Independent SQF/HACCP/PCQI consultants in the US bill roughly $95-$350/hr (most $100-$150), $1,300-$2,000/day benchmarks, ~$20k-$55k per full SQF system build, $10k-$28k per HACCP plan, and offer "eConsultant" retainers at 25% discounts to day rates; retainer-heavy firms exist (a Boston lab/consultancy derives 85% of $751k revenue from retainers with 5 staff), but nearly all pricing evidence is forum anecdote and no dataset of solo food-safety consultant income was found.

### Cited Findings (all IFSQN figures are forum anecdotes)
- Full SQF program build quotes: $35,000 USD (May 2023, SQFconsultant); another consultant quoted $55,000 and $28,000 for two facilities; "average development project: about $20,000, covering up to 30 days on site" (SQFconsultant, Dec 2018); IFSQN's own template packages "start at less than $1,000USD per site" (vendor claim) — [IFSQN thread 45793](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/45793-how-much-should-i-charge-for-creating-a-whole-food-safety-program-and-get-the-production-facility-certified-with-gmp-audit/); [IFSQN thread 42613](https://ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/42613-sqf-training-and-consulting); [IFSQN thread 27713](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/27713-what-is-your-food-safety-consultant-charging)
- HACCP plan development: $10-15k per plan (associate's price); $15-20k for a small bakery; $28,000 for a HACCP plan document including travel — [IFSQN 48846](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/48846-haccp-plan-development-cost); [IFSQN 47459](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/47459-fair-price-to-charge-for-developing-2-haccp-plans/)

- J.J. Keller independent-contractor food safety consultant postings: $100-$130/hr; one listing shows $200k-$260k/yr (site's own estimate of market range $80k-$150k) — [J.J. Keller job](https://jobs.jjkeller.com/job/Neenah-Independent-Contractor-Food-Safety-Consultant-WI-54956/1121180500/); [Jobicy listing](https://jobicy.com/jobs/145000-independent-contractor-food-safety-consultant.md)

- Retainer structure: Glenn Oster Consulting "eConsultant Retainer" gives 25% off day/project rates; "a simple subscription eConsultant could easily save your company over 20,000" (no price disclosed) — [IFSQN 27713](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/27713-what-is-your-food-safety-consultant-charging); [IFSQN 43669](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/43669-freelancer-consultant-pricing); unsourced pricing article cites retainers of $1,500-$5,000/month — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-much-should-you-pay-for-food-safety-audit-subscriptions) (low credibility)

- Firm examples: Comprehensive Food Safety (NJ) claims 700+ client projects; ASC Food Safety offers "once-off consulting fee OR a retainer fee"; Naperville firm sells FSPCA PCQI classes plus SQF/BRC/FSSC audit prep — [SQFI training center directory](https://www.sqfi.com/directories/training-center-directory/24B0F400000073-Comprehensive-Food-Safety); [ASC Food Safety](https://ascfoodsafety.com/food-safety-consulting-company/); [Food Safety Magazine directory](https://www.food-safety.com/directories/205-buyer-s-guide/listing/467-bd-food-safety-consultants-llc)

- Consolidation signal: NSF acquired Euro Consultants Group and Davis Fresh Technologies (food safety consultancies) — [Crain's Detroit](https://prod.crainsdetroit.com/node/55111); [Food Processing](https://www.foodprocessing.com/on-the-plant-floor/plant-operations-and-engineering/news/11343190/production-operations-nsf-international-acquires-davis-fresh-technologies-food-processing)
- Alan Weiss: fees depend only on "value provided in the perception of the buyer" and ethical intent; "consultants, not clients, are the main cause of low consulting fees"; time-and-materials is "lunacy" — [getAbstract summary of Value-Based Fees](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/value-based-fees/43481)
- Built to Sell (Warrillow): sellable firm must be teachable, valuable (specialized), repeatable (recurring revenue); he converted his own consultancy to subscription before selling in 2008 — [Mixergy interview](https://mixergy.com/interviews/john-warrillow-built-sell-interview/)
- Local cluster (Magic Valley): Chobani Twin Falls plant 1,200 workers, $500M expansion to 1.6M sq ft (2025); Clif Bar 275,000 sq ft bakery ($90M); Glanbia processes 12M lb milk/day (one-third of Idaho's milk) with $82M Gooding/Twin Falls expansion; Clear Springs Foods, Amalgamated Sugar research, Fabri-Kal cup plant in Burley; ~6,000 direct/indirect jobs attributed to Chobani/Clif and suppliers — [BoiseDev 2025](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/03/20/food-manufacturer-groundbreaking-magic-valley-march-2025/); [Trade & Industry Development](https://www.tradeandindustrydev.com/industry/food-agriculture-related/news/id-chobani-100m-investment-and-6000-jobs-fueling-m-11720); [Dairy Herd on Glanbia](https://www.dairyherd.com/news/business/glanbia-foods-announces-82-million-expansion-southern-idaho); [Idaho Business Review](https://idahobusinessreview.com/?p=229402)

### Inferences
- At $150/hr and ~1,000 billable hours/yr, a solo practice grosses ~$150k; at project pricing ($20k-$35k per SQF build) 4-6 builds a year reaches similar numbers with less time-selling. Both are consistent with the $80k-$150k "market range" estimate and the J.J. Keller $100-$130/hr contractor rates.
- The Boston listing shows what a mature retainer firm looks like: ~$750k revenue, 5 staff, ~20% cash-flow margin, and heavy owner dependence (the Built to Sell problem).
- Being physically in the Magic Valley cluster is a real edge: on-site day rates are 40% above remote, and the cluster contains dozens of SQF/FSSC-certified plants within an hour's drive (inference from the plant list; no count of certified sites was found).

### Gaps
- No survey or dataset of solo food-safety consultant income; everything is anecdote or job postings.
- No evidence on how independents actually acquire clients (referral vs auditor networks vs trade shows); IFSQN "transition to consulting" thread did not address it.
- No count of GFSI-certified plants in southern Idaho was found.

## KQ5. Fractional roles (fractional QA manager / SQF practitioner / CI lead): retainers, whether small plants buy, where demand is listed

### Takeaway
Fractional QA/quality roles price at roughly $4,400-$9,100/month (~13 hrs/week at $85-$175/hr) per Go Fractional's benchmark, and remote "SQF practitioner" contract postings exist (20-30 hrs/wk for 3-4 months, with site visits), but the SQF Code requires the practitioner to be employed by the site, so the viable structure is "fractional overseer + trained employee as designated practitioner/substitute."

### Cited Findings
- Go Fractional QA Manager benchmark: $4,400-$9,100/month at 13 hrs/week; average $144/hr across live benchmarks; notes insufficient role-specific data — [Go Fractional](https://www.gofractional.com/insights/rates/quality-assurance-manager); fractional process-improvement manager from $5,000/month for 5-10 hrs/week — [Go Fractional](https://www.gofractional.com/hire/process-improvement-manager)
- Fractional CRO for manufacturers $5k-$15k/month (10-30 hrs/wk); manufacturing-specific expertise (ISO 9001, lean) "commands a premium" — [Pulse RevOps](https://pulserevops.com/tools/tl21207)
- Remote SQF Practitioner contract posting (food distributor pursuing SQF): initial site visit plus periodic follow-ups, 20-30 hrs/week for 3-4 months — [Atrium Staffing](https://atriumstaff.com/?p=64423); ingredient blender posting: remote within commuting distance, backup practitioner on site — [WayUp](https://www.wayup.com/i-j-Quality-Specialist-Professions-Inc-818389367639302/)
- SQF Code Edition 9 requires each site to designate a primary practitioner and at least one substitute — [Zosi Learning](https://blog.zosilearning.com/sqf-substitute-practitioners-explained); forum consensus citing the code: practitioner must "be employed by the site" and hold management responsibility; a consultant "can be neither" primary nor backup; auditors expect the site practitioner to lead, and one auditor intervened when the practitioner deferred to a consultant; unannounced audits must begin within 30 minutes of arrival so a practitioner must be on site in business hours — [IFSQN 47034](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/47034-seeking-advice-on-transitioning-to-a-consulting-role)
- Part-time arrangements accepted in practice: a WA winery's practitioner works two days/week with a HACCP-trained bottling manager as backup, accepted by its CB; a consultant describes one regional practitioner overseeing several sites each with a full-time HACCP-qualified backup — [IFSQN 31951](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/31951-sqf-practitioner-in-winery-vintage); [IFSQN 32489](https://ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/32489-sqf-backupdesignate-requirements)
- One UK consultant declined a pro-rata offer of ~£20k/yr for three days a week (anecdote on how plants try to price part-time arrangements) — [IFSQN 27713](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/27713-what-is-your-food-safety-consultant-charging)
- SafetyCulture "fractional" alternative: an SQF "eConsultant" subscription model exists (see KQ4) — [IFSQN 43669](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/43669-freelancer-consultant-pricing)

### Inferences
- A defensible fractional offer is "fractional FSQA director + audit-readiness retainer" at ~$4k-$9k/month per plant, with the plant's own employee named as practitioner. Two to three such retainers would replace a mid-level plant salary in Idaho (county median household income ~$65k-$73k, KQ9).

## KQ8. Side business while employed: Idaho non-compete law (44-2701 et seq.), employee IP assignment, moonlighting and conflict of interest (e.g., selling to Darigold's competitors)

### Takeaway
Idaho enforces post-employment non-competes only for "key employees" (rebuttably presumed if in the top 5% of pay) and presumes 18 months/line-of-business/geography reasonable (Idaho Code 44-2704, last amended 2018 to repeal the 2016 irreparable-harm presumption); Idaho has no California-style employee-invention statute, so the employer's IP-assignment clause governs side-project software; and regardless of contract, the common-law duty of loyalty makes competing with or selling to the employer's competitors while employed the highest-risk activity.

### Cited Findings
- Idaho Code 44-2704 text (current): (1) no post-employment restriction "exceeds a period of eighteen (18) months... unless consideration, in addition to employment or continued employment, is given"; (2)-(4) rebuttable presumptions of reasonableness for <=18 months, geography where the employee "provided services or had a significant presence or influence," and the "type of employment or line of business conducted by the key employee"; (5) "an employee or independent contractor who is among the highest paid five percent (5%) of the employer's employees... is a 'key employee'"; rebut by showing "no ability to adversely affect the employer's legitimate business interests"; history: added 2008, am. 2016, am. 2018 — [Idaho Legislature 44-2704](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title44/T44CH27/SECT44-2704/)
- 44-2701 general standard: covenant must be reasonable and not "impose a greater restraint than is reasonably necessary to protect the employer's legitimate business interests"; 44-2702 defines key employee by employer's investment of "time, money, trust, or exposure" producing "high level of inside knowledge, influence, credibility"; legitimate interests include customers, customer lists, referral sources, vendors, financial/marketing info, trade secrets; 44-2703 allows blue-penciling; Idaho courts treat covenants as disfavored and requiring narrow tailoring — [Holland & Hart (2012)](https://www.hollandhart.com/restrictive-covenants-in-idaho)
- 2018 repeal (SB 1287, effective March 28, 2018, became law without governor's signature) removed the 2016 "rebuttable presumption of irreparable harm" against key employees; employers must again prove likelihood of irreparable harm for an injunction — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/utah-and-idaho-enact-employee-friendly-amendments-to-non-compete-legislation)
- Employee-invention statutes exist in CA, DE, IL, KS, MN, NC, WA (near-identical), plus NV, UT, NJ, NY (2023); Idaho is not among them in any source reviewed — [Foley & Lardner](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/); [Epstein Becker Green on NY](https://www.ebglaw.com/insights/publications/new-york-restricts-assignment-of-employee-intellectual-property); "If a state doesn't have a California-style statute, the contract may have more reach" — [terms.law](https://terms.law/2025/07/04/employees-side-projects-who-really-owns-what/)
- Utah (neighboring) statute distinguishes "employment inventions" from own-time inventions (Utah Code 34-39-1) — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/722830/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitfalls)
- Side-project risk factors: "Use of company tools, work hours, or confidential data increases employer claims to side project IP" — [Aaron Hall](https://aaronhall.com/?p=450040)
- Duty of loyalty: "in most states employees owe a duty of loyalty... would forbid an employee from moonlighting for a competitor or running a business that competes with their employer"; non-competitive after-hours work "is less likely to be upheld as a breach" absent competition, confidentiality or proprietary issues; with a non-compete, competitor moonlighting "could lead from 1 job to 2, and quickly to 0 jobs" — [Zegal](https://zegal.com/blog/post/my-star-employee-is-moonlighting-for-someone-else-in-the-evening-is-that-legal); [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/?p=47076)
- Employees creating work within scope of employment: employer is author/owner without assignment (work-for-hire for employees) — [Gordon Feinblatt](https://gfrlaw.com/what-we-do/insights/work-made-hire-misunderstood-copyright-concept)

### Inferences
- A plant QA/CI professional is plausibly in the top 5% of pay at a dairy plant and would be presumed a "key employee"; an 18-month, dairy-line-of-business, Magic Valley-area non-compete would be presumptively enforceable in Idaho. The founder should obtain and read the signed agreement before any outreach to Glanbia/Chobani/Darigold-competitor plants.
- Software built for the employer's own plant on personal time, if it "relates to the employer's business," is at high risk of being claimed under a typical assignment clause because Idaho offers no statutory carve-out. Building a generic tool on personal equipment, documenting dates, and getting a written carve-out or license from the employer are the mitigations the sources recommend.
- Selling anything to Darigold's direct competitors while employed by Darigold is the scenario the duty-of-loyalty literature singles out; selling to non-competing food segments (bakery, potato, trout, sugar) in the same cluster carries far less risk.

### Gaps
- Darigold's actual code of conduct / outside-employment policy was not found online; must be obtained from HR/handbook.
- No Idaho case law on employee side-project software ownership was found.
- Whether Idaho courts enforce a non-compete against someone who voluntarily leaves to serve non-competing food segments is fact-specific; no source addressed it.

### Inferences
- The regulated-signature constraint (PCQI must prepare/oversee the food safety plan; SQF practitioner must be site-employed) means an AI-assisted service here is a throughput multiplier on a consultant's project pricing ($20k-$35k SQF builds done in fewer days), not a labor-replacement SaaS; this favors value/project pricing (Weiss, Stark) over hourly.
- VC "services-as-software" theses assume venture scale and are not evidence about solo economics; a16z's margin warnings (humans-in-loop, per-customer customization) describe exactly the plant-by-plant customization a solo operator will face.

### Gaps
- No example found of a solo food-safety consultant publicly running an AI-enabled productized service with disclosed pricing or throughput.

## KQ13. The single highest-leverage first move for this profile

### Takeaway
The evidence converges on one move: convert an existing plant relationship into a paid, scoped engagement (pilot, build, or audit-readiness retainer) with a signed LOI/agreement that fixes price and IP, before writing any more product code — this is Walling's step 1/2, Limble's post-failure lesson, the Vegam/FoodDocs origin pattern, and the only path that produces revenue while employed; the legal gating item is reading the employer's non-compete/IP clause first.

### Cited Findings
- Walling: step 1 is a simple, sellable product with one traffic channel; owning your time (replacement income) comes before SaaS; "there's a long ramp to any kind of substantial revenue" — [MicroConf Stair Step](https://microconf.com/latest/the-stairstep-approach-to-bootstrapping); he would consult nights/weekends and keep the day job — [SFTROU ep. 610](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-610-how-i-would-start-over-today-bad-habits-of-solopreneurs-and-the-benefits-of-a-day-job-a-rob-solo-adventure)

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence strength: (1) one paid engagement with a non-competing local plant (bakery/potato/trout/sugar/nutrition-bar segments to avoid dairy-competitor conflict), priced as a project or monthly retainer, with an LOI and IP-carve-out; (2) join one or two vendor referral/consultant programs to monetize the same relationships; (3) speaking/newsletter (IMPA, IFSQN webinars) as the single traffic channel feeding (1). SaaS is step 3, funded by (1)-(2), consistent with every bootstrapped manufacturing story found.
- Acquisition (KQ10) is a credible alternative only after leaving the day job and with ~$20k-$50k liquid.
